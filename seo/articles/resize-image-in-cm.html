A digital image has no size in centimetres on its own: it has pixels, plus a resolution (dots per inch) that says how big to print them. That is why “resize to 3.5 × 4.5 cm” really means “resize to the right number of pixels and store the right dpi”. The tool does both, and keeps the file under a KB limit if the form has one.

How to Convert cm to Pixels

Pixels = centimetres ÷ 2.54 × dpi. So 3.5 cm at 200 dpi is 3.5 ÷ 2.54 × 200 = 276 pixels. The table below has the common sizes worked out; the tool does the same sum when you choose “Set width and height in cm”.

When a form gives only centimetres and no dpi, 200 dpi is a safe choice for an on-screen upload. Use 300 dpi if the picture will be printed, for example on a card.

Printing at the Right Size

When you print, choose “Actual size” or 100% scale, not “Fit to page”. Because the dpi is stored in the file, a 276 × 354 pixel photo saved at 200 dpi prints at exactly 3.5 × 4.5 cm. Frequently Asked Questions: Resize Image to CM

How do I resize an image to 3.5 × 4.5 cm?

Use the tool above: it is preset to 3.5 × 4.5 cm at 200 dpi (276 × 354 pixels). Add the photo and download.

How many pixels is 1 cm?

It depends on the dpi: about 39 pixels at 100 dpi, 79 at 200 dpi and 118 at 300 dpi.

How do I resize an image in mm?

Divide the millimetres by ten and enter them as centimetres: 35 × 45 mm is 3.5 × 4.5 cm.

What dpi should I use?

200 dpi for most online forms, 300 dpi for anything that will be printed. 100 dpi gives a very small file but looks soft when printed.

Can I resize to inches?

Multiply inches by 2.54 to get centimetres: a 2 × 2 inch photo is 5.08 × 5.08 cm, or 600 × 600 pixels at 300 dpi.
