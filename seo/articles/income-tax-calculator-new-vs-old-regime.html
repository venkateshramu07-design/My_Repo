Updated 27 October 2026

Quick answer on income tax calculator: For income earned between 1 April 2026 and 31 March 2027, the new tax regime charges nothing up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income (₹12.75 lakh of salary, after the ₹75,000 standard deduction), then 15% to 30% on higher slabs. The old regime has higher rates but lets you deduct 80C investments, health insurance, HRA and home loan interest. For most people the new regime is cheaper; the old one usually wins only if your deductions add up to well over ₹4–5 lakh a year. Enter your own numbers below to see both side by side.

Budget 2026 left the slabs, the rebate and the standard deduction unchanged from last year. The bigger change is the law itself: the Income-tax Act, 2025 replaced the 1961 Act from 1 April 2026, so many familiar section numbers have new numbers (the rebate, long known as Section 87A, is now Section 156). The tax you pay at a given income is the same.

A rebate of up to ₹60,000 wipes out the tax completely when taxable income is ₹12 lakh or less. Just above ₹12 lakh, marginal relief caps your tax at the amount by which your income exceeds ₹12 lakh, so earning ₹10,000 more never costs you ₹60,000 in tax. The relief stops mattering at about ₹12.7 lakh. Salaried people and pensioners also get a ₹75,000 standard deduction, and employer contributions to NPS of up to 14% of basic pay are deductible. Almost nothing else is.

How Income Tax Is Calculated, Step by Step

  1. Start with your gross salary and other income for the year.
  2. Subtract the standard deduction: ₹75,000 in the new regime, ₹50,000 in the old one. In the old regime, also subtract deductions such as 80C, 80D, HRA and home loan interest.
  3. Apply the slab rates to the remaining taxable income, one slab at a time.
  4. Subtract the section 87A rebate if you qualify: in the new regime, tax is nil up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income (with marginal relief just above it); in the old regime, up to ₹5 lakh.
  5. Add any surcharge on high incomes, then 4% health and education cess on the total.

In the old regime the rebate is ₹12,500, so tax is zero only up to ₹5 lakh of taxable income, and the standard deduction is ₹50,000. In both regimes a 4% health and education cess is added to the tax, and a surcharge applies above ₹50 lakh (10%), ₹1 crore (15%) and ₹2 crore (25%; 37% above ₹5 crore in the old regime only).

Which Deductions Only the Old Regime Allows

  • 80C, up to ₹1.5 lakh: your PF contribution, PPF, ELSS mutual funds, life insurance premiums, Sukanya Samriddhi, five-year tax-saving FDs, home loan principal and children's tuition fees.
  • 80D: health insurance premiums, up to ₹25,000 for yourself and family (₹50,000 if you are a senior citizen), plus the same again for parents.
  • HRA exemption if you pay rent and get house rent allowance.
  • Home loan interest on a self-occupied house, up to ₹2 lakh.
  • 80CCD(1B): an extra ₹50,000 of your own NPS contributions.
  • Smaller ones: education loan interest (80E), donations (80G), savings account interest up to ₹10,000 (80TTA), professional tax.

When the Old Regime Still Wins

The calculator shows the break-even point for your income: how much more you would need in deductions before the old regime becomes cheaper. As a rough guide for a salaried person under 60:

  • At ₹10–12.75 lakh of salary, the new regime is almost always better, because its tax is zero.
  • At ₹15 lakh, deductions of roughly ₹5.5 lakh are needed before the old regime wins. With only the full 80C and some health insurance, the new regime saves about ₹1 lakh a year.
  • At ₹25 lakh and above, the old regime can win for people with large HRA, a home loan and full 80C and NPS. Run your numbers rather than guessing.

Two practical points: the new regime is the default, so you must choose the old one explicitly when you file. Salaried people can switch every year; people with business or professional income can move back from the old regime only once.

Worked Example

Priya earns a ₹15 lakh salary and ₹20,000 of bank interest. She puts ₹1.5 lakh into PF and PPF and pays ₹25,000 for health insurance.

  • New regime: ₹15.2 lakh minus ₹75,000 = ₹14.45 lakh taxable. Tax ₹96,750, plus 4% cess = ₹1,00,620.
  • Old regime: ₹15.2 lakh minus ₹50,000, ₹1.5 lakh and ₹25,000 (and ₹2,500 professional tax) = ₹12.925 lakh taxable. Tax ₹2,00,250, plus cess = ₹2,08,260.

The new regime saves her ₹1,07,640 a year. She would need about ₹3.7 lakh more in deductions for the old regime to win.

Frequently Asked Questions: Income Tax Calculator

Is income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free in FY 2026-27?

Yes, in the new regime, for resident individuals, because the ₹60,000 rebate cancels the tax on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh. Salaried people can earn ₹12.75 lakh because of the ₹75,000 standard deduction. The rebate does not cover income taxed at special rates, such as some capital gains.

Did Budget 2026 change the tax slabs?

No. The new regime slabs, the ₹12 lakh rebate limit and the ₹75,000 standard deduction stayed the same as for FY 2025-26. The Income-tax Act, 2025 took effect from 1 April 2026 and renumbered many sections, but did not change these rates.

Can I switch between the old and new regime?

If you have no business income, yes, every year when you file your return, whatever you told your employer. With business or professional income, you can opt out of the new regime and return to it only once.

What is marginal relief?

It stops a small rise in income from causing a big jump in tax. In the new regime, if your taxable income is just over ₹12 lakh, your tax is capped at the amount above ₹12 lakh: on ₹12.1 lakh you pay ₹10,000 plus cess, not ₹61,500. A similar relief applies at each surcharge level.

Is employer NPS deductible in the new regime?

Yes. Your employer's contribution to your NPS account is deductible up to 14% of basic pay plus DA in the new regime (10% in the old regime). Your own NPS contributions are deductible only in the old regime.

Does this calculator include capital gains?

No. Gains on shares, mutual funds and property are taxed at special rates and need to be added separately. The calculator covers salary, pension, interest, rent and other income taxed at slab rates.
