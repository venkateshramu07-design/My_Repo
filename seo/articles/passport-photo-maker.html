Quick answer on passport photo maker: You can make a passport or visa photo at home with a phone, a plain light wall and daylight. Choose your country below, add your photo, move it until your face fits the oval, and download either a digital file for an online application or a 4 × 6 inch sheet of photos to print at any photo kiosk for very little. The tool uses each government's official size and head-height rules, works for the US, UK, Schengen countries, Canada, Australia, China and India's OCI, and runs on your device, so your photo is never uploaded.

Passport photo shops and apps typically charge $15 to $20 for two prints, and many "free" apps send your face to their servers and charge at the download. A passport photo is one of the most sensitive images you own: it is exactly what identity thieves need. Australia's passport office even warns against online photo services for that reason.

How to Take a Passport Photo at Home

  1. Find a plain wall, white or very light grey, and stand about half a metre (two feet) in front of it so your shadow falls away from it.
  2. Face a window in daylight. Even light from the front stops shadows on one side of your face. Turn off the flash.
  3. Ask someone else to take it from about 1.5 metres (five feet) away, phone at your eye level. Selfies distort the face and many countries don't accept them.
  4. Look straight at the camera with a neutral expression, mouth closed, eyes open, hair away from your eyes, no hat (except for religious or medical reasons) and, for US photos, no glasses.
  5. Take several shots and pick the sharpest, then add it to the tool above.

Print or Digital: Which Do You Need?

  • Online applications (US visas and the DV lottery, UK online passports, Canadian online renewal, China's online visa form, OCI) need a digital file. Choose "A digital photo to upload" and the tool saves the exact pixels and file size.
  • Paper applications need printed photos. Choose "Printed photos": you get a 4 × 6 inch (10 × 15 cm) sheet with as many copies as fit. Print it at 100% scale at a supermarket or pharmacy photo kiosk, then cut along the lines. A single 4 × 6 print usually costs well under $1.

Two exceptions: Canada asks for printed photos with the photo studio's name and address on the back, and Australia requires professional prints rather than home inkjet prints. Their pages explain what to do.

Why Photos Get Rejected

  • The head is too small or too large for the frame (the oval in the tool prevents this).
  • Shadows on the face or behind the head, or a coloured or patterned background.
  • Glare on glasses, tinted lenses, or (for the US) any glasses at all.
  • Blurry, grainy or over-compressed images.
  • Filters, beauty mode, background removal or other edits: governments reject altered photos.
  • Photos older than six months, or that no longer look like you.
  • Printing "fit to page", which shrinks the photo. Always print at 100%.

Frequently Asked Questions: Passport Photo Maker

Can I smile in a passport photo?

Most countries, including the US, UK, Canada, Australia and the Schengen states, require a neutral expression with your mouth closed. The US allows a natural, closed-mouth smile; a wide smile showing teeth gets photos rejected almost everywhere.

Can I wear makeup in a passport photo?

Yes, everyday makeup is fine as long as you still look like yourself. Avoid heavy contouring, glitter or anything that changes your features, and remember glasses are not allowed in US passport photos.

Is there an app to make passport photos?

Many apps do it, but most upload your face to their servers. This free tool works in your phone's browser instead, so your photo never leaves your device.

Can I take my own passport photo with my phone?

Yes for most countries, as long as it meets the rules. The UK and US accept photos taken at home; many countries recommend that someone else takes it rather than a selfie. Canada and Australia have printing rules described on their pages.

How do I print passport photos cheaply?

Download the 4 × 6 inch sheet from the tool and print it at a photo kiosk or online photo lab as a standard 4 × 6 (10 × 15 cm) print at actual size. You get several passport photos from one print.

Is my photo uploaded anywhere?

No. The photo is opened, cropped and saved inside your browser on your own phone or computer. Nothing is sent to Blog Digest or anyone else.

Does the tool remove the background?

No, on purpose. Most governments don't accept photos with edited backgrounds. Instead the tool checks whether your background is plain and light enough and tells you if it isn't.

What if my country isn't listed?

Choose "Other: enter the size" and type the width, height and head size from your application's instructions. For exam and job application forms, use the [[post:articles/exam-photo-signature-resizer|exam photo and signature resizer]].
