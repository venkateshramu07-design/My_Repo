Quick answer: For an OCI (Overseas Citizen of India) application you upload a square colour photo as a JPEG, at least 200 × 200 pixels, and most Indian missions say no larger than 900 × 900 pixels and 200 KB, with a plain light background and the full face visible from the front. Some missions allow up to 1,500 pixels and 500 KB, but a 600 × 600 pixel file under 200 KB meets every version of the rules. The OCI portal is known for rejecting photos without a clear reason, usually because of file size, a non-square image or a busy background. The rules also differ slightly between the Indian embassies and consulates that handle applications, so the safest choice is a file that fits the strictest version.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the OCI photo size?

A square JPEG of at least 200 × 200 pixels; most missions set a maximum of 900 × 900 pixels and 200 KB. A 600 × 600 pixel file under 200 KB is accepted everywhere.

Why is my OCI photo rejected?

The usual causes are a file over the size limit, an image that isn't square, a busy or dark background, or a face that is too small. The tool handles the first three and checks the background.

Can I use my passport photo for OCI?

Only if it's recent and square. Most passport photos are rectangular (35 × 45 mm), so they need re-cropping to a square, which the tool does.

What background should an OCI photo have?

Plain and light. Some missions say white, others ask for a light colour that isn't pure white, so a very light grey or off-white wall is the safest choice.
