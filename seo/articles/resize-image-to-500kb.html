How to Resize an Image to 500KB in 4 Steps

  1. Choose your photo in the tool above (or several at once). iPhone HEIC photos and PNG screenshots are fine; the result is always a real JPG.
  2. Check the limit: the tool is set to 500 KB. If the form also gives a minimum, such as “100 to 500 KB”, type it in the second box so the file is not too small either.

  4. Download the JPG (or all of them as a .zip) and upload it. The result line shows the exact KB, the size in pixels and the JPEG quality it used.

500 KB or 1 MB?

If a form allows 1 MB, a 500 KB photo still passes and uploads twice as fast on a slow connection. Going lower than you need has no benefit, though: set the tool to the form's actual limit for the best quality. The chips under the size box switch instantly between 10 KB and 1 MB and re-compress every photo you have added.

How the Tool Picks the Size

A JPEG's file size depends on two things: how many pixels it has and how strongly it is compressed (the “quality” setting). Most compressors only lower the quality, which is why their 20 KB results look blocky. This tool searches for the highest quality that fits and, if that would fall below about 60%, makes the photo slightly smaller in pixels instead, because a smaller sharp photo looks far better on a form than a large blurry one. It also writes the resolution (dpi) into the file, which some government portals check, and counts 1 KB as 1,000 bytes so the file passes whichever way the portal measures.

How do I compress a photo to 500 KB?

Add it to the tool above with the limit at 500 KB and download the JPG.

Is 500 KB high quality?

Yes, for almost any photo: a 500 KB JPEG can keep a 12-megapixel photo at its full size with only light compression.

How do I reduce an image from MB to KB?

Choose the image and set the KB you need; the tool handles a 10 MB photo the same way as a 1 MB one. Use the chips for common sizes.

How many KB is half an MB?

500 KB if you count 1,000 KB per MB (phones and Macs), or 512 KB if you count 1,024 (Windows).

Can I keep the original pixel size?

Usually, yes, at 500 KB. The result line shows the pixels; if they are smaller than the original, the photo needed it to stay sharp under 500 KB.
