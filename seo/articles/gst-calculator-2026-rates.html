Quick answer: To add GST, multiply the price by the rate: ₹10,000 at 18% is ₹1,800 of GST, ₹11,800 in total. To take GST out of a price that already includes it, divide by 1 plus the rate: ₹11,800 ÷ 1.18 = ₹10,000, so the GST was ₹1,800. Since 22 September 2025 most goods and services fall into three rates, 5%, 18% and 40%, plus 0% for essentials and 3% for gold. In the same state the tax is split equally into CGST and SGST; across states or on imports it is charged as IGST. The calculator below does all of this and shows the split for your invoice.

The GST 2.0 reform folded the old 12% and 28% slabs into the others, so many prices and invoices changed overnight. Most packaged food, medicines and everyday items moved down to 5%, most other goods and services sit at the 18% standard rate, and a new 40% rate covers luxury and "sin" goods.

A common mistake is to take 18% off an inclusive price: 18% of ₹11,800 is ₹2,124, which is wrong. The GST inside an 18% inclusive price is always 18/118, about 15.25%, of that price.

The rate depends on the exact HSN code (for goods) or SAC code (for services), not on the general category, so two similar-looking products can carry different rates. Before you invoice, check the item on the CBIC rate list or with your accountant.

CGST, SGST and IGST: Which to Show on the Invoice

  • Seller and buyer in the same state: charge CGST and SGST, each at half the rate. 18% becomes 9% CGST (to the Centre) plus 9% SGST (to the state). In a union territory without a legislature, SGST is called UTGST.
  • Seller and buyer in different states, or imports: charge IGST at the full rate, 18% in one line.
  • The total tax is the same either way; only the split and the government that receives it change.

Do You Need to Register for GST?

Registration becomes compulsory once your yearly turnover passes ₹40 lakh for most sellers of goods and ₹20 lakh for services (lower limits of ₹20 lakh and ₹10 lakh apply in some special-category states). Selling to other states, through e-commerce marketplaces in many cases, or providing online services can require registration from the first rupee. Small businesses can choose the composition scheme, paying a low flat rate on turnover but without charging GST on invoices or claiming input credit.

Frequently Asked Questions: GST Calculator

How do I calculate GST in reverse?

To take GST out of a price that already includes it, divide by 1 plus the rate. For 18% GST: price ÷ 1.18 gives the amount before GST, and the difference is the tax. Choose “Remove GST” in the calculator above to do it for any rate.

Is this GST calculator for India?

Yes. It uses India's GST slabs, splits the tax into CGST and SGST for sales within a state or IGST for sales between states, and works for any amount.

How do I calculate GST from a total amount?

Divide the total by 1 plus the rate to get the price before GST, then subtract. For ₹1,180 at 18%: ₹1,180 ÷ 1.18 = ₹1,000, so GST is ₹180. Use "Remove GST from a price" in the calculator.

What happened to the 12% and 28% GST rates?

From 22 September 2025, under the GST 2.0 reform, most items at 12% moved to 5% and most items at 28% moved to 18%, with luxury and sin goods moved to a new 40% rate.

Is GST charged on GST?

No. GST is charged on the value of the goods or services, including other charges such as delivery or packing that are part of the sale, but not on the GST itself.

Why does my restaurant bill show 5% GST?

Restaurant service (outside hotels with high room rates) is taxed at 5% without input tax credit, split into 2.5% CGST and 2.5% SGST.

How is GST rounded on an invoice?

Tax is worked out to the paisa and is usually rounded to the nearest rupee on the final invoice total. The calculator shows amounts to two decimal places so you can round as your billing software does.
