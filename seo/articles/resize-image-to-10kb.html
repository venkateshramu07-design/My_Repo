Signature Under 10 KB: What Works

Sign with a black gel pen on plain white paper, photograph it flat in daylight and crop close to the ink. In our test, a signature photographed on paper (380 KB) came out at 9.7 KB and 838 × 377 pixels in black and white, which is sharp enough to zoom into. In colour, the same file had to drop to about half the width, because the grey paper texture takes up space.

If the form fixes the signature at, say, 140 × 60 pixels, choose “Set width and height in pixels”. At that size a clean signature is often only 3–6 KB even at top quality, and the tool can add an invisible blank block to reach a minimum such as 4 KB.

How the Tool Picks the Size

A JPEG's file size depends on two things: how many pixels it has and how strongly it is compressed (the “quality” setting). Most compressors only lower the quality, which is why their 20 KB results look blocky. This tool searches for the highest quality that fits and, if that would fall below about 60%, makes the photo slightly smaller in pixels instead, because a smaller sharp photo looks far better on a form than a large blurry one. It also writes the resolution (dpi) into the file, which some government portals check, and counts 1 KB as 1,000 bytes so the file passes whichever way the portal measures.

Frequently Asked Questions: Resize Image to 10KB

How do I reduce a photo to 10 KB?

Use the tool above: choose the photo, keep the limit at 10 KB and download. On a phone without the tool, you would have to crop the photo, then shrink it to about 400 pixels wide in an editing app and save it at medium quality, checking the size each time.

Is 10 KB enough for a photo?

For a small on-screen photo, yes: about 400 × 300 pixels at good quality, or around 270 × 350 for a passport-style crop. It is too small to print. Check that the form really asks for 10 KB for the photo and not only for the signature.

How many pixels is a 10 KB image?

It depends on the picture. In our tests, the detailed camera photo fitted at about 270 × 220 pixels, the smoother 12-megapixel image at about 400 × 300, and a black-and-white signature at about 840 × 380, all at 65–70% JPEG quality.

Why does the portal say my 10 KB file is too large?

Windows counts 1 KB as 1,024 bytes; some portals count 1,000. A file Windows shows as 9.9 KB can be 10,140 bytes, which a 10,000-byte limit rejects. This tool keeps files under 10,000 bytes, so they pass either way.

Can I resize a signature to 10 KB without losing quality?

Mostly, yes: tick “Black and white” and crop tightly. A clean black signature on white compresses very well, so it keeps far more detail than a colour photo of the same size.
