Quick answer: Term life insurance is cheap when you are young and healthy and becomes steeply more expensive with age. Across 21 major US insurers, a $500,000 20-year term policy for a non-smoker in average health costs on average about $38 a month for a 30-year-old man and $32 for a woman, $59 and $47 at 40, $137 and $102 at 50, and $395 and $286 at 60, according to MoneyGeek's October 2026 survey. People in excellent health can pay 40% to 50% less, and smokers typically pay two to three times as much. Premiums more than double with every ten years of age after 40, so buying earlier, and only for the years your family depends on your income, is the biggest way to save. A common rule of thumb is cover of 10 to 15 times your income, but the calculator below gives a more precise figure based on your debts, savings and family's needs.

Most life insurance price pages are run by brokers that want your contact details before showing a number. This guide shows typical prices openly, explains what drives them and how much cover most families need, and includes a calculator that estimates both your cover and your premium without asking for any personal details.

Two patterns stand out. First, the price rises slowly until about 40 and then accelerates: a 60-year-old man pays more than ten times as much as a 30-year-old for the same cover. Second, women pay less at every age, from a few per cent in their 20s to about 20% to 30% from 40 onwards, because they live longer on average.

These are averages for "standard" health. Insurers sort applicants into classes such as preferred plus, preferred, standard plus and standard; a healthy 40-year-old man in the best class might pay about $28 a month for the same policy, according to InsuranceGeek's 2026 rate data, less than half the average.

What Determines Your Premium

  • Age: the single biggest factor. Each year you wait adds to the cost, and health problems that appear with age can push you into a more expensive class.
  • Health: insurers look at your weight, blood pressure, cholesterol, medical history and often a blood test. Conditions such as diabetes or heart disease raise prices or lead to exclusions.
  • Smoking and nicotine: usually doubling or tripling the premium. Many insurers treat vaping and nicotine replacement the same as smoking. If you quit, you can usually reapply at non-smoker rates after 12 months.
  • Cover amount and term: doubling the cover roughly doubles the premium, although larger policies are often slightly cheaper per unit. A 30-year term costs more than a 20-year term because it covers older ages.
  • Family history, occupation and hobbies: dangerous jobs, private flying, diving or motor racing can add loadings.
  • Sex: in the US and many countries, women pay less. In the European Union, insurers are not allowed to charge men and women different prices, so prices are unisex.

How Much Cover Do You Need?

Life insurance exists to replace what your family would lose if you died. A simple and widely used approach adds up four things and subtracts what you already have:

  1. Income: your take-home pay multiplied by the number of years your family would need it, for example until your youngest child finishes education.
  2. Debts: the mortgage and other loans your family would want to pay off.
  3. Future costs: education, childcare, and funeral costs.
  4. Minus savings, investments and any existing cover, including workplace death-in-service benefits, which are often two to four times salary.

For a parent with $60,000 take-home pay, 10 years of income need, a $250,000 mortgage and other debts, $50,000 for future costs and $40,000 of savings and existing cover, that comes to about $850,000. At 35, a non-smoking man in average health would pay roughly $80 a month for that amount over 20 years.

The "10 to 15 times income" rule of thumb gives a similar answer for many families, but the calculation is better because it accounts for your debts and savings. If you have no dependants and no shared debts, you may not need life insurance at all.

Term or Whole Life?

The prices above are for term insurance, which pays out only if you die within the term and has no cash value. Whole life and universal life insurance last your whole life and build cash value, but cost far more: often 5 to 15 times as much for the same cover.

  • Term insurance suits most families, because the need for cover is usually greatest while children are young and the mortgage is large, and falls as savings grow.
  • Whole life insurance can make sense for specific needs, such as lifelong dependants, estate planning in countries with high inheritance taxes, or business succession. Our guide [[post:articles/inheritance-tax-by-country-2026]] covers when estates are taxed.

The common advice to "buy term and invest the difference" works only if you actually invest the difference consistently. Many people find the discipline of whole life useful; most find term plus a pension or index fund cheaper.

Ways to Pay Less

  1. Buy younger. Premiums for level term are fixed for the whole term, so locking in at 30 rather than 40 can cut your lifetime cost substantially.
  2. Match the term to your need. A 20-year term may cover the years until your children are independent; a 30-year term costs more but matches a 30-year mortgage.
  3. Consider decreasing cover for a repayment mortgage, which is cheaper because the payout falls as the debt does.
  4. Stop smoking and reapply after 12 months nicotine-free.
  5. Compare several insurers or use an independent broker. Prices for the same person can vary by 50% or more between companies, and insurers assess health conditions differently.
  6. Pay annually if offered a discount, and check your workplace benefits before buying more cover than you need.
  7. Be honest on the application. Non-disclosure is the most common reason claims are refused.

Life Insurance Outside the US

Term life insurance works the same way in most countries, but prices and rules differ:

  • United Kingdom: level and decreasing term policies are widely sold alongside mortgages, and putting the policy in trust usually keeps the payout outside the estate for inheritance tax and speeds up payment.
  • European Union: prices must be the same for men and women. Mortgage lenders in several countries, including France and Spain, require borrower insurance.
  • Canada and Australia: term cover is often held inside workplace plans or, in Australia, inside superannuation, where many members have default cover they may not know about.
  • India: pure term plans are inexpensive and premiums qualify for tax deductions under the old tax regime.

Check whether you already have cover through your employer, pension or mortgage before buying more.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is life insurance per month?

For a healthy non-smoker buying $500,000 of 20-year term cover, about $30 to $40 a month in your 20s and early 30s, $47 to $59 at 40 and $100 to $140 at 50, on average. People in excellent health can pay less.

How much life insurance do I need?

Enough to replace your income for the years your family would need it, plus your debts and future costs, minus savings and existing cover. For many families this comes to 10 to 15 times income.

At what age is life insurance most expensive?

Premiums rise every year and accelerate after 40; at 60, cover costs about ten times as much as at 30. Many insurers stop offering new 20-year terms in your late 60s or 70s.

Is term life insurance worth it?

If anyone depends on your income or would have to repay shared debts if you died, usually yes: it is one of the cheapest ways to protect a family. If you have no dependants or debts, you may not need it.

Do smokers pay more for life insurance?

Yes, typically two to three times as much. Most insurers offer non-smoker rates once you have been nicotine-free for 12 months.

Does life insurance cost the same for men and women?

In the US and many other countries, women pay less, by about 20% to 30% from age 40. In the EU, prices must be the same for both sexes.

Final Thoughts

For a young, healthy parent, a large amount of term cover costs less than a streaming subscription or two, and it is among the most valuable financial protections a family can have. The two decisions that matter most are how much cover you need and how long you need it for; the cheapest time to buy is usually now.
