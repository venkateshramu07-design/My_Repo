Quick answer on traveling with medication internationally: Usually yes, but not everywhere, and some places need a permit before you fly. Japan bans Adderall, Vyvanse and other amphetamine ADHD medicines outright, as well as cold tablets with pseudoephedrine such as Sudafed. The UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Singapore, South Korea and the Philippines want you to get approval online before travelling with codeine, tramadol, sleeping pills, anxiety medicine or ADHD medicine. CBD oil is illegal in the Gulf states, Singapore, South Korea, China and Hong Kong. For most other medicine, carrying your prescription and no more than one to three months' supply in the original packet is enough.

The rules follow the active ingredient, not the brand, and they are set by each country separately, which is why a medicine you buy over the counter at home can get you detained elsewhere. Pick your destination and medicine below.

The Countries Where Travellers Get Caught Out Most

Japan

Japan is the strictest common destination. Amphetamine medicines (Adderall, Vyvanse, Dexedrine) are classed as stimulants and cannot be brought in even with a prescription. Over-the-counter cold medicines containing pseudoephedrine, such as Sudafed and Actifed, and Vicks inhalers are also banned. Opioid painkillers need a permit from a Regional Narcotics Control Department before you travel, and products containing codeine sold over the counter abroad are not allowed. For ordinary prescription medicine, up to a month's supply is fine; more needs an Import Confirmation (Yunyu Kakunin-sho), applied for by email before you leave.

United Arab Emirates

Codeine, tramadol, sleeping pills, anxiety medicine and ADHD medicine are controlled. The Ministry of Health (MOHAP) gives free prior approval online, or you can declare the medicine on arrival with your prescription and a medical report. Either way, bring no more than three months' supply. Passengers who only change planes in Dubai or Abu Dhabi don't need approval.

Saudi Arabia and Qatar

Saudi Arabia's Food and Drug Authority (SFDA) asks travellers with controlled medicine to apply for a clearance permit in its Controlled Drugs System, with a prescription or report from the last six months. You may bring 30 days' supply or the length of your stay, whichever is shorter; this applies to Hajj and Umrah pilgrims too. Qatar opened a similar online permit system in August 2026; apply at least five working days before you travel.

Singapore and South Korea

Singapore lets you bring up to three months' supply without approval, unless the medicine contains an ingredient on the Health Sciences Authority's controlled list, in which case you need HSA approval first. South Korea requires approval from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety for any medicine with a narcotic or psychotropic ingredient, whatever your nationality; you can apply online. A foreign prescription alone is not enough in either country.

Thailand and Indonesia

Thailand needs a Thai FDA permit, applied for online at least 15 days ahead, for narcotic medicines, and limits controlled medicine to 30 days' supply. Amphetamine is a Category 1 narcotic there. Indonesia treats ADHD medicine such as dexamphetamine as an illegal drug, and the Australian government warns that codeine, morphine and sleeping pills can be treated the same way without proper paperwork; drug penalties in Indonesia are among the harshest in the world.

Europe

If you travel between Schengen countries with a narcotic or psychotropic medicine, ask your doctor or pharmacist for a Schengen certificate (under Article 75 of the Schengen Agreement), which covers up to 30 days. Coming from outside the Schengen area, carry the prescription and a doctor's letter, ideally translated into English. Some countries add national rules: Finland, for example, treats lisdexamfetamine (Vyvanse) as a narcotic.

How to Travel With Restricted Medicine Safely

  1. Check the active ingredient on the packet, not the brand name. The same brand can contain different ingredients in different countries.
  2. Apply early. Permits take from a few minutes (UAE) to two weeks or more (Japan, Thailand). Start a month before you fly.
  3. Carry the paperwork in your hand luggage: the prescription, a doctor's letter with your name, the diagnosis, the dose and the generic name of the medicine, and any permit.
  4. Keep medicine in its original, labelled packaging, and bring no more than you need for the trip.
  5. Declare it at customs when the country asks (red channel), even if you hold a permit.
  6. Check transit countries if you leave the airport or collect your bags between flights.
  7. Don't post medicine ahead. Mailing controlled medicine is banned or needs separate licences almost everywhere.

The International Narcotics Control Board keeps a list of each country's rules for travellers, and your destination's embassy can confirm anything not covered here.

Frequently Asked Questions: Traveling with Medication Internationally

Is it okay to carry pills in a Ziploc bag when flying?

Within your own country it is usually fine. Abroad, keep prescription medicine in its original labelled packaging with the prescription, because loose pills are hard for customs to identify and can be confiscated.

Can I take Adderall to Japan?

No. Adderall contains amphetamine, which Japan bans even with a prescription. Talk to your doctor about an alternative for the trip well before you travel.

Can I take codeine to Dubai?

Yes, with paperwork. Codeine is controlled in the UAE, so get MOHAP approval online before you travel or declare it on arrival with your prescription and a medical report, and bring no more than three months' supply.

Can I take sleeping pills abroad?

In most countries, yes, with your prescription and a reasonable supply. The UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Singapore and South Korea want approval before you arrive, and Indonesia may treat them as illegal drugs without paperwork.

Is CBD oil allowed on international flights?

It is illegal in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Singapore, South Korea, China, Hong Kong and Indonesia, and products with THC are banned in Japan. Leave it at home unless you have checked both ends of your trip.

How much medicine can I take abroad?

Usually no more than you need for the trip: one month in Japan and Qatar, 30 days of controlled medicine in Thailand and Saudi Arabia, three months in the UAE, Singapore, South Korea and the UK, and around 90 days for the US.
