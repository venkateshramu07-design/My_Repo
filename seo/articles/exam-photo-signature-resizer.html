How to Take a Photo That Passes

  • Use a plain white or very light wall and stand about a metre in front of it so there is no shadow behind your head.
  • Face a window or a soft light. Light from one side makes half your face dark; light from behind makes your whole face dark.
  • Look straight at the camera with a neutral expression, both ears visible and no cap or dark glasses. NTA asks for about 80% of the frame to be your face.
  • Hold the phone at eye level, at arm's length or more. Close-up selfies stretch the nose and make the face look distorted.
  • Take a recent photo. Several exams now compare your uploaded photo with a live photo taken on the day, so an old photo can cause trouble at the centre.

In the tool, drag the photo so your face sits in the middle of the frame with a little space above your head, and use the zoom so your head and the top of your shoulders fill most of it. "Brighten a dull or dark photo" helps if the picture came out grey.

Getting the Signature Right

  1. Sign on plain white paper with a black ink pen (most exams ask for black; check yours). Gel pens give the clearest line.
  2. Sign in your usual running hand. Banks and the SSC reject signatures written entirely in capital letters.
  3. Photograph the paper flat, in daylight, from directly above, without your shadow on it.
  4. In the tool, choose Signature, crop tightly around the signature and leave "Clean up: dark ink on a pure white background" ticked. It removes the grey paper tone and makes the ink crisp, which also keeps the file small.

The same steps work for the left thumb impression (use a blue or black ink pad and press once, without smudging) and for the handwritten declaration, which must be in your own handwriting, in English, and not typed.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I reduce my photo to 20–50 KB for an exam form?

Choose your exam and "Photograph" in the resizer above, add the photo, position your face in the frame and download. The tool saves the exact pixel size and the best quality between 20 and 50 KB.

What is 3.5 cm × 4.5 cm in pixels?

It depends on the print resolution: about 138 × 177 pixels at 100 dpi, 276 × 354 at 200 dpi and 413 × 531 at 300 dpi. If a form gives centimetres only, the 200 dpi size (around 275 × 354) is a safe choice, and the tool writes the dpi into the file.

Are JPG and JPEG the same?

Yes. They are two names for the same format, and forms that ask for one accept the other. Renaming a PNG to .jpg does not convert it, though, and portals can detect that; the tool saves a genuine JPG.

My signature file is only 6 KB but the form needs at least 10 KB. What do I do?

Use the tool: when a clean signature compresses below the minimum, it adds an invisible blank block inside the JPG so the file reaches the required size while the image stays the same.

Can I use a photo taken on my phone?

Yes, as long as it follows the rules: recent, white background, face straight on, good light. Phone photos are much larger than forms allow, which is exactly what the resizer fixes.

Does the form check the dpi?

A few do, especially for thumb impressions (200 dpi is common). The tool writes 200 or 300 dpi into the file depending on the exam, which is what those checks read.
