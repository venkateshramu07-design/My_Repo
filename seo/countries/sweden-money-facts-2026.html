Minimum Wage in Sweden (2026)

Sweden has no statutory national minimum wage. Pay floors are set sector by sector in collective agreements between unions and employers, which cover most workers and are often higher than the legal minimums of neighbouring countries.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Sweden

A single worker on the average wage in Sweden (about $66,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 22.6% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 77%. That is 10th lowest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 22.6% of pay at the average wage, 19.6% at two-thirds of it and 32.7% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 41.1% of labour costs.

Electricity Prices in Sweden

Households in Sweden pay about €0.306 per kWh of electricity (about $0.34), 7th most expensive of 33 countries; the EU average is €0.283. Change: +12.9% vs late 2025.

Household gas: €0.220 per kWh. Typical household using 2,500–5,000 kWh a year, all taxes included, January–June 2026.

Petrol Prices in Sweden Since the Iran War

Petrol in Sweden costs €1.64/L (week of 19 October 2026), up 14.4% from €1.43/L in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at €1.77/L (23 Mar). At about $1.84 a litre it is 4th cheapest of 29 countries, and the rise is 4th smallest of 29 countries.

Retirement Age in Sweden

The state pension age in Sweden is 67 (guarantee pension) for people reaching it in 2026; the earliest you can claim is 64 (income pension). Future change: Ages linked to life expectancy. The earnings-related income pension can be drawn from 64; the 'target age' is 67.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Sweden

A full-career average earner in Sweden can expect a mandatory pension worth about 66.3% of their previous net pay, 25th most generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 67%. High earner (twice the average): 84%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Sweden

Bank deposits in Sweden are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.

Sweden has no inheritance tax for children. Abolished in 2005.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Sweden have a minimum wage?

No. Sweden has no national minimum wage set by law; minimum pay is agreed sector by sector in collective agreements.

How much tax do you pay in Sweden?

On the average wage, income tax plus employee social security in Sweden take 22.6% of gross pay, so a single worker keeps 77% (OECD average: 25.1%).

What is the average pay rise in Sweden for 2027?

Pay surveys forecast an average salary increase of 3.0% in Sweden for 2027, against inflation of %.

How much does electricity cost in Sweden?

Household electricity in Sweden costs about €0.306 per kWh (about $0.34), 7th most expensive of 33 countries.

How much has petrol gone up in Sweden?

Petrol in Sweden has risen 14.4% since February 2026, from €1.43/L to €1.64/L.

What is the retirement age in Sweden?

The state pension age in Sweden is 67 (guarantee pension) in 2026, with the earliest claim at 64 (income pension). Legislated change: Ages linked to life expectancy.

How much is the state pension in Sweden?

In Sweden, mandatory pensions replace about 66.3% of an average earner's net pay after a full career, against an OECD average of 63.2%.

How much money is insured in a bank in Sweden?

Deposits in Sweden are insured up to €100,000 per person, per bank.

Is there inheritance tax in Sweden?

No. Sweden does not tax inheritances passed to children. Abolished in 2005.
