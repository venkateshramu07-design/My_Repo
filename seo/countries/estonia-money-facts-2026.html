Minimum Wage in Estonia (2026)

The legal minimum wage in Estonia is €946/month. For a month of full-time work that is about $1,059 at current exchange rates, 16th lowest of 38 countries in our comparison.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Estonia

A single worker on the average wage in Estonia (about $43,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 23.2% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 77%. That is 13th lowest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 23.2% of pay at the average wage, 15.7% at two-thirds of it and 23.2% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 42.6% of labour costs.

Mortgage Rates in Estonia (2026)

The typical mortgage rate in Estonia is 4.27% (new home loans, August 2026), 9th highest of 31 countries; the euro area average is 3.6%.

Up 0.48 points since February 2026 (3.79%). Monthly payment per €100,000 over 25 years: €543.

Electricity Prices in Estonia

Households in Estonia pay about €0.212 per kWh of electricity (about $0.24), 13th cheapest of 33 countries; the EU average is €0.283. Change: −8.0% vs late 2025.

Household gas: €0.080 per kWh. Typical household using 2,500–5,000 kWh a year, all taxes included, January–June 2026.

Petrol Prices in Estonia Since the Iran War

Petrol in Estonia costs €1.95/L (week of 19 October 2026), up 34.5% from €1.45/L in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at €1.95/L (19 Oct). At about $2.19 a litre it is 12th most expensive of 29 countries, and the rise is 5th largest of 29 countries.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Estonia

A full-career average earner in Estonia can expect a mandatory pension worth about 37.8% of their previous net pay, 4th least generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 56%. High earner (twice the average): 24%. Including common voluntary private pensions, an average earner would get 63%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Estonia

Bank deposits in Estonia are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.
