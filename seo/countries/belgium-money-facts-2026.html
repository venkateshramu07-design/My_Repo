Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Belgium

A single worker on the average wage in Belgium (about $88,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 39.5% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 60%. That is the highest of 38 countries we compare, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 39.5% of pay at the average wage, 31.5% at two-thirds of it and 47.4% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 52.5% of labour costs.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Belgium

A full-career average earner in Belgium can expect a mandatory pension worth about 61.1% of their previous net pay, 23rd least generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 81%. High earner (twice the average): 42%. Including common voluntary private pensions, an average earner would get 74%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Belgium

Bank deposits in Belgium are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.

Inheritance Tax in Belgium

Belgium has inheritance tax (regional). Children can receive Low; varies by region tax-free, and the top rate for children is 27%, 9th highest of 28 countries. Flanders 3% to 27% for children; Brussels and Wallonia have higher top rates.
