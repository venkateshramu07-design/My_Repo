Minimum Wage in Slovakia (2026)

The legal minimum wage in Slovakia is €915/month. For a month of full-time work that is about $1,024 at current exchange rates, 14th lowest of 38 countries in our comparison.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Slovakia

A single worker on the average wage in Slovakia (about $38,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 24.3% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 76%. That is 17th lowest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 24.3% of pay at the average wage, 21.5% at two-thirds of it and 26.9% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 42.7% of labour costs.

Mortgage Rates in Slovakia (2026)

The typical mortgage rate in Slovakia is 3.73% (new home loans, August 2026), 13th highest of 31 countries; the euro area average is 3.6%.

Up 0.35 points since February 2026 (3.38%). Monthly payment per €100,000 over 25 years: €513.

Electricity Prices in Slovakia

Households in Slovakia pay about €0.199 per kWh of electricity (about $0.22), 8th cheapest of 33 countries; the EU average is €0.283. Change: +7.1% vs late 2025.

Household gas: €0.063 per kWh. Typical household using 2,500–5,000 kWh a year, all taxes included, January–June 2026.

Petrol Prices in Slovakia Since the Iran War

Petrol in Slovakia costs €1.89/L (week of 19 October 2026), up 27.9% from €1.47/L in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at €1.89/L (19 Oct). At about $2.11 a litre it is 14th cheapest of 29 countries, and the rise is 12th largest of 29 countries.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Slovakia

A full-career average earner in Slovakia can expect a mandatory pension worth about 76.3% of their previous net pay, 17th most generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 86%. High earner (twice the average): 68%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Slovakia

Bank deposits in Slovakia are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.
