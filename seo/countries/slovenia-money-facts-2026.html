Minimum Wage in Slovenia (2026)

The legal minimum wage in Slovenia is €1,482/month. For a month of full-time work that is about $1,659 at current exchange rates, 11th highest of 38 countries in our comparison.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Slovenia

A single worker on the average wage in Slovenia (about $54,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 36.2% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 64%. That is 4th highest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 36.2% of pay at the average wage, 33.0% at two-thirds of it and 39.7% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 45.3% of labour costs.

Households in Slovenia pay about €0.212 per kWh of electricity (about $0.24), 12th cheapest of 33 countries; the EU average is €0.283. Change: −0.1% vs late 2025.

Petrol Prices in Slovenia Since the Iran War

Petrol in Slovenia costs €1.75/L (week of 19 October 2026), up 23.3% from €1.42/L in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at €1.76/L (5 Oct). At about $1.96 a litre it is 6th cheapest of 29 countries, and the rise is 14th smallest of 29 countries.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Slovenia

A full-career average earner in Slovenia can expect a mandatory pension worth about 71.3% of their previous net pay, 22nd most generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 100%. High earner (twice the average): 73%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Slovenia

Bank deposits in Slovenia are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.
