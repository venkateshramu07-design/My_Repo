Minimum Wage in Denmark (2026)

Denmark has no statutory national minimum wage. Pay floors are set sector by sector in collective agreements between unions and employers, which cover most workers and are often higher than the legal minimums of neighbouring countries.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Denmark

A single worker on the average wage in Denmark (about $88,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 35.3% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 65%. That is 5th highest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 35.3% of pay at the average wage, 32.5% at two-thirds of it and 41.6% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 35.8% of labour costs.

Electricity Prices in Denmark

Households in Denmark pay about €0.237 per kWh of electricity (about $0.27), 16th cheapest of 33 countries; the EU average is €0.283. Change: −28.3% vs late 2025.

Household gas: €0.121 per kWh. Typical household using 2,500–5,000 kWh a year, all taxes included, January–June 2026.

Petrol Prices in Denmark Since the Iran War

Petrol in Denmark costs €2.60/L (week of 19 October 2026), up 34.4% from €1.94/L in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at €2.66/L (12 Oct). At about $2.92 a litre it is the most expensive of 29 countries we compare, and the rise is 6th largest of 29 countries.

Retirement Age in Denmark

The state pension age in Denmark is 67 for people reaching it in 2026; the earliest you can claim is Early pension for worn-out workers. Future change: 68 (2030), 69 (2035), 70 (2040). A May 2025 law sets 70 for people born after 31 December 1970, the highest in Europe.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Denmark

A full-career average earner in Denmark can expect a mandatory pension worth about 77.1% of their previous net pay, 16th most generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 117%. High earner (twice the average): 64%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Denmark

Bank deposits in Denmark are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.

Inheritance Tax in Denmark

Denmark has estate duty. Children can receive About DKK 340,000 tax-free, and the top rate for children is 15%, 12th highest of 28 countries. Flat 15% above the exemption for children; spouses exempt.

Frequently Asked Questions: Denmark Minimum Wage 2026

Does Denmark have a minimum wage?

No. Denmark has no national minimum wage set by law; minimum pay is agreed sector by sector in collective agreements.

How much tax do you pay in Denmark?

On the average wage, income tax plus employee social security in Denmark take 35.3% of gross pay, so a single worker keeps 65% (OECD average: 25.1%).

How much does electricity cost in Denmark?

Household electricity in Denmark costs about €0.237 per kWh (about $0.27), 16th cheapest of 33 countries.

How much has petrol gone up in Denmark?

Petrol in Denmark has risen 34.4% since February 2026, from €1.94/L to €2.60/L.

What is the retirement age in Denmark?

The state pension age in Denmark is 67 in 2026, with the earliest claim at Early pension for worn-out workers. Legislated change: 68 (2030), 69 (2035), 70 (2040).

How much is the state pension in Denmark?

In Denmark, mandatory pensions replace about 77.1% of an average earner's net pay after a full career, against an OECD average of 63.2%.

How much money is insured in a bank in Denmark?

Deposits in Denmark are insured up to €100,000 per person, per bank.

Is there inheritance tax in Denmark?

Yes. Children can inherit About DKK 340,000 tax-free in Denmark; above that the top rate for children is 15%.
