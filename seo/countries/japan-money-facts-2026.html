Minimum Wage in Japan (2026)

The legal minimum wage in Japan is ¥1,177/hour (average). For a month of full-time work that is about $1,301 at current exchange rates, 14th highest of 38 countries in our comparison. National weighted average for fiscal 2026; set by prefecture, from ¥1,280 in Tokyo.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Japan

A single worker on the average wage in Japan (about $60,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 22.6% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 77%. That is 11th lowest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 22.6% of pay at the average wage, 20.6% at two-thirds of it and 27.0% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 33.1% of labour costs.

Pay Rises in Japan: 2027 Forecast

Employers in Japan expect to raise salaries by 3.0% in 2027 (median of the major pay surveys), after 3.1% in 2026. With inflation at 1.7% (Aug, core), that is a real rise of about 1.3 points.

Estimate by Mizuho Research after the Bank of Japan raised its rate to 1.25% in September 2026; fixed rates about 3.5%.

Retirement Age in Japan

The state pension age in Japan is 65 for people reaching it in 2026; the earliest you can claim is 60 (reduced). Future change: none legislated. Basic pension can start any time from 60 to 75, with reductions or increases.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Japan

A full-career average earner in Japan can expect a mandatory pension worth about 42.4% of their previous net pay, 7th least generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 62%. High earner (twice the average): 34%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Japan

Bank deposits in Japan are protected up to ¥10 million plus interest per depositor, per bank (about $63,800), under the DICJ scheme. Non-interest settlement accounts fully protected. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.

Inheritance Tax in Japan

Japan has inheritance tax. Children can receive ¥30 million + ¥6 million per heir (whole estate) tax-free, and the top rate for children is 55%, the highest of 28 countries we compare. Rates rise from 10% to 55% above ¥600 million per heir's share; spouses get a large deduction.
