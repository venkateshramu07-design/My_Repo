Minimum Wage in Latvia (2026)

The legal minimum wage in Latvia is €780/month. For a month of full-time work that is about $873 at current exchange rates, 11th lowest of 38 countries in our comparison.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Latvia

A single worker on the average wage in Latvia (about $43,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 26% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 74%. That is 17th highest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 26.0% of pay at the average wage, 22.4% at two-thirds of it and 28.9% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 40.1% of labour costs.

Mortgage Rates in Latvia (2026)

The typical mortgage rate in Latvia is 4.35% (new home loans, August 2026), 8th highest of 31 countries; the euro area average is 3.6%.

Up 0.38 points since February 2026 (3.97%). Monthly payment per €100,000 over 25 years: €547.

Electricity Prices in Latvia

Households in Latvia pay about €0.241 per kWh of electricity (about $0.27), 16th most expensive of 33 countries; the EU average is €0.283. Change: −1.5% vs late 2025.

Household gas: €0.081 per kWh. Typical household using 2,500–5,000 kWh a year, all taxes included, January–June 2026.

Petrol Prices in Latvia Since the Iran War

Petrol in Latvia costs €1.95/L (week of 19 October 2026), up 27.2% from €1.53/L in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at €2.04/L (28 Sep). At about $2.18 a litre it is 14th most expensive of 29 countries, and the rise is 13th largest of 29 countries.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Latvia

A full-career average earner in Latvia can expect a mandatory pension worth about 52.2% of their previous net pay, 14th least generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 64%. High earner (twice the average): 51%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Latvia

Bank deposits in Latvia are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.
