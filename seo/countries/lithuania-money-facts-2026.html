Minimum Wage in Lithuania (2026)

The legal minimum wage in Lithuania is €1,153/month. For a month of full-time work that is about $1,291 at current exchange rates, 15th highest of 38 countries in our comparison.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Lithuania

A single worker on the average wage in Lithuania (about $57,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 38.7% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 61%. That is 3rd highest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 38.7% of pay at the average wage, 33.5% at two-thirds of it and 39.5% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 39.8% of labour costs.

The typical mortgage rate in Lithuania is 4.16% (new home loans, August 2026), 10th highest of 31 countries; the euro area average is 3.6%.

Up 0.49 points since February 2026 (3.67%). Monthly payment per €100,000 over 25 years: €537.

Electricity Prices in Lithuania

Households in Lithuania pay about €0.216 per kWh of electricity (about $0.24), 14th cheapest of 33 countries; the EU average is €0.283. Change: +10.6% vs late 2025.

Household gas: €0.063 per kWh. Typical household using 2,500–5,000 kWh a year, all taxes included, January–June 2026.

Petrol Prices in Lithuania Since the Iran War

Petrol in Lithuania costs €1.95/L (week of 19 October 2026), up 33.8% from €1.46/L in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at €1.95/L (28 Sep). At about $2.18 a litre it is 13th most expensive of 29 countries, and the rise is 8th largest of 29 countries.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Lithuania

A full-career average earner in Lithuania can expect a mandatory pension worth about 28.2% of their previous net pay, 2nd least generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 37%. High earner (twice the average): 21%. Including common voluntary private pensions, an average earner would get 50%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Lithuania

Bank deposits in Lithuania are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.
