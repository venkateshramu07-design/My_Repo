Minimum Wage in Poland (2026)

The legal minimum wage in Poland is €1,119/month. For a month of full-time work that is about $1,252 at current exchange rates, 17th highest of 38 countries in our comparison.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Poland

A single worker on the average wage in Poland (about $54,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 24.4% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 76%. That is 18th lowest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 24.4% of pay at the average wage, 22.6% at two-thirds of it and 29.3% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 35.0% of labour costs.

Households in Poland pay about €0.300 per kWh of electricity (about $0.34), 8th most expensive of 33 countries; the EU average is €0.283. Change: +10.7% vs late 2025.

Petrol Prices in Poland Since the Iran War

Petrol in Poland costs €1.56/L (week of 19 October 2026), up 13.3% from €1.38/L in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at €1.85/L (28 Sep). At about $1.75 a litre it is 3rd cheapest of 29 countries, and the rise is 2nd smallest of 29 countries.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Poland

A full-career average earner in Poland can expect a mandatory pension worth about 40.6% of their previous net pay, 6th least generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 41%. High earner (twice the average): 37%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Poland

Bank deposits in Poland are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.
