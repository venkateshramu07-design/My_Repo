Minimum Wage in Austria (2026)

Austria has no statutory national minimum wage. Pay floors are set sector by sector in collective agreements between unions and employers, which cover most workers and are often higher than the legal minimums of neighbouring countries.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Austria

A single worker on the average wage in Austria (about $86,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 32.5% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 68%. That is 7th highest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 32.5% of pay at the average wage, 26.9% at two-thirds of it and 37.1% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 47.1% of labour costs.

Mortgage Rates in Austria (2026)

The typical mortgage rate in Austria is 3.61% (new home loans, August 2026), 16th lowest of 31 countries; the euro area average is 3.6%.

Up 0.18 points since February 2026 (3.43%). Monthly payment per €100,000 over 25 years: €507.

Electricity Prices in Austria

Households in Austria pay about €0.326 per kWh of electricity (about $0.36), 4th most expensive of 33 countries; the EU average is €0.283. Change: −0.4% vs late 2025.

Household gas: €0.124 per kWh. Typical household using 2,500–5,000 kWh a year, all taxes included, January–June 2026.

Petrol Prices in Austria Since the Iran War

Petrol in Austria costs €1.82/L (week of 19 October 2026), up 20.4% from €1.51/L in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at €1.96/L (28 Sep). At about $2.03 a litre it is 12th cheapest of 29 countries, and the rise is 7th smallest of 29 countries.

Retirement Age in Austria

The state pension age in Austria is 65 men / 61 y 6 m women for people reaching it in 2026; the earliest you can claim is 62 (corridor pension). Future change: 65 for women by 2033. Women's age is rising by six months a year.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Austria

A full-career average earner in Austria can expect a mandatory pension worth about 86.8% of their previous net pay, 8th most generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 85%. High earner (twice the average): 62%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Austria

Bank deposits in Austria are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.

Inheritance Tax in Austria

Austria has no inheritance tax for children. Abolished in 2008; property transfer tax still applies.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does Austria have a minimum wage?

No. Austria has no national minimum wage set by law; minimum pay is agreed sector by sector in collective agreements.

How much tax do you pay in Austria?

On the average wage, income tax plus employee social security in Austria take 32.5% of gross pay, so a single worker keeps 68% (OECD average: 25.1%).

What are mortgage rates in Austria right now?

Mortgage rates in Austria are about 3.61% (new home loans, August 2026). Up 0.18 points since February 2026 (3.43%). Monthly payment per €100,000 over 25 years: €507.

How much does electricity cost in Austria?

Household electricity in Austria costs about €0.326 per kWh (about $0.36), 4th most expensive of 33 countries.

How much has petrol gone up in Austria?

Petrol in Austria has risen 20.4% since February 2026, from €1.51/L to €1.82/L.

What is the retirement age in Austria?

The state pension age in Austria is 65 men / 61 y 6 m women in 2026, with the earliest claim at 62 (corridor pension). Legislated change: 65 for women by 2033.

How much is the state pension in Austria?

In Austria, mandatory pensions replace about 86.8% of an average earner's net pay after a full career, against an OECD average of 63.2%.

How much money is insured in a bank in Austria?

Deposits in Austria are insured up to €100,000 per person, per bank.

Is there inheritance tax in Austria?

No. Austria does not tax inheritances passed to children. Abolished in 2008; property transfer tax still applies.
