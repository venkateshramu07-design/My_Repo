Minimum Wage in the United States (2026)

The legal minimum wage in the United States is $7.25/hour. For a month of full-time work that is about $1,257 at current exchange rates, 16th highest of 38 countries in our comparison. $7.25 an hour, unchanged since 2009; 30 states and DC set higher rates, up to $17.13 in Washington state.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in the United States

A single worker on the average wage in the United States (about $74,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 24.3% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 76%. That is 16th lowest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 24.3% of pay at the average wage, 21.4% at two-thirds of it and 29.2% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 30.0% of labour costs.

Pay Rises in the United States: 2027 Forecast

Employers in the United States expect to raise salaries by 3.4% in 2027 (median of the major pay surveys), after 3.4% in 2026. With inflation at 3.7% (Sep), that is a real cut of about 0.3 points unless inflation falls.

Mortgage Rates in the United States (2026)

The typical mortgage rate in the United States is 7.32% (30-year fixed, 22 Oct 2026), 3rd highest of 31 countries.

Freddie Mac average; up from 5.98% in late February. Per $100,000 over 30 years: $687 a month.

Electricity Prices in the United States

Households in the United States pay about 18.3¢ per kWh of electricity (about $0.18), 6th cheapest of 33 countries. Basis: National average, July 2026.

EIA average residential price, up 4.9% on a year earlier; ranges from about 11¢ to over 40¢ by state.

Petrol Prices in the United States Since the Iran War

Petrol in the United States costs $4.28/gal (week of 19 October 2026), up 45.9% from $2.94/gal in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at $4.50/gal (11 May). At about $1.13 a litre it is the cheapest of 29 countries we compare, and the rise is the largest of 29 countries we compare.

Retirement Age in the United States

The state pension age in the United States is 67 for people reaching it in 2026; the earliest you can claim is 62 (reduced). Future change: none legislated. Full retirement age is 67 for anyone born in 1960 or later. Each year you delay up to 70 adds 8% to the benefit.

How Much the State Pension Pays in the United States

A full-career average earner in the United States can expect a mandatory pension worth about 51.3% of their previous net pay, 13th least generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 62%. High earner (twice the average): 40%. Including common voluntary private pensions, an average earner would get 101%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in the United States

Bank deposits in the United States are protected up to $250,000 per depositor, per bank, under the FDIC scheme. Per ownership category; joint and retirement accounts insured separately. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.

Inheritance Tax in the United States

The United States has federal estate tax. Children can receive $15 million per person tax-free, and the top rate for children is 40%, 5th highest of 28 countries. Exemption made permanent and indexed by the 2025 tax law; about a dozen states and DC add their own estate or inheritance taxes.

Frequently Asked Questions: US Minimum Wage 2026

What is the minimum wage in the United States in 2026?

The minimum wage in the United States is $7.25/hour, about $1,257 a month for full-time work. That is 16th highest of 38 countries. $7.25 an hour, unchanged since 2009; 30 states and DC set higher rates, up to $17.13 in Washington state.

How much tax do you pay in the United States?

On the average wage, income tax plus employee social security in the United States take 24.3% of gross pay, so a single worker keeps 76% (OECD average: 25.1%).

What is the average pay rise in the United States for 2027?

Pay surveys forecast an average salary increase of 3.4% in the United States for 2027, against inflation of 3.7%.

What are mortgage rates in the United States right now?

Mortgage rates in the United States are about 7.32% (30-year fixed, 22 Oct 2026). Freddie Mac average; up from 5.98% in late February. Per $100,000 over 30 years: $687 a month.

How much does electricity cost in the United States?

Household electricity in the United States costs about 18.3¢ per kWh (about $0.18), 6th cheapest of 33 countries.

How much has petrol gone up in the United States?

Petrol in the United States has risen 45.9% since February 2026, from $2.94/gal to $4.28/gal.

What is the retirement age in the United States?

The state pension age in the United States is 67 in 2026, with the earliest claim at 62 (reduced). Legislated change: None legislated.

How much is the state pension in the United States?

In the United States, mandatory pensions replace about 51.3% of an average earner's net pay after a full career, against an OECD average of 63.2%.

How much money is insured in a bank in the United States?

Deposits in the United States are insured up to $250,000 per person, per bank.

Is there inheritance tax in the United States?
