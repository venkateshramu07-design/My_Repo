Minimum Wage in Czechia (2026)

The legal minimum wage in Czechia is €923/month. For a month of full-time work that is about $1,033 at current exchange rates, 15th lowest of 38 countries in our comparison.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in Czechia

A single worker on the average wage in Czechia (about $45,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 21.3% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 79%. That is 8th lowest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 21.3% of pay at the average wage, 18.7% at two-thirds of it and 23.4% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 41.2% of labour costs.

Electricity Prices in Czechia

Households in Czechia pay about €0.288 per kWh of electricity (about $0.32), 9th most expensive of 33 countries; the EU average is €0.283. Change: −10.4% vs late 2025.

Household gas: €0.090 per kWh. Typical household using 2,500–5,000 kWh a year, all taxes included, January–June 2026.

Petrol Prices in Czechia Since the Iran War

Petrol in Czechia costs €1.86/L (week of 19 October 2026), up 35.7% from €1.37/L in the week before the Iran war began. It peaked at €1.88/L (21 Sep). At about $2.08 a litre it is 13th cheapest of 29 countries, and the rise is 3rd largest of 29 countries.

Retirement Age in Czechia

The state pension age in Czechia is 64 y 6 m for people reaching it in 2026; the earliest you can claim is Up to 5 years early (reduced). Future change: 67 by 2056. Age depends on birth year; a 2024 law sets the path to 67.

How Much the State Pension Pays in Czechia

A full-career average earner in Czechia can expect a mandatory pension worth about 55.9% of their previous net pay, 22nd least generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 84%. High earner (twice the average): 40%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in Czechia

Bank deposits in Czechia are protected up to €100,000 per depositor, per bank (about $111,900), the limit set by EU law for every member state. Harmonised by EU law; local-currency equivalent outside the euro. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.
