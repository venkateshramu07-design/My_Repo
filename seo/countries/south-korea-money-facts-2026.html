Minimum Wage in South Korea (2026)

The legal minimum wage in South Korea is ₩10,320/hour. For a month of full-time work that is about $1,351 at current exchange rates, 13th highest of 38 countries in our comparison. 2026 rate.

Income Tax and Take-Home Pay in South Korea

A single worker on the average wage in South Korea (about $68,000 at purchasing-power parity) pays 16.5% of gross pay in income tax and employee social security, so they keep 83%. That is 5th lowest of 38 countries, against an OECD average of 25.1%.

Income tax and employee social security take 16.5% of pay at the average wage, 13.3% at two-thirds of it and 20.9% at 1.67 times it. Including employer contributions, the total tax wedge is 24.8% of labour costs.

Retirement Age in South Korea

The state pension age in South Korea is 63 for people reaching it in 2026; the earliest you can claim is 58 (reduced). Future change: 64 in 2027; 65 by 2033. National Pension age depends on birth year.

How Much the State Pension Pays in South Korea

A full-career average earner in South Korea can expect a mandatory pension worth about 38.9% of their previous net pay, 5th least generous of 50 countries (OECD average 63.2%). Low earner (half the average wage): 55%. High earner (twice the average): 25%.

How Much of Your Money Is Protected in South Korea

Bank deposits in South Korea are protected up to ₩100 million per depositor, per bank (about $75,500), under the KDIC scheme. Doubled from ₩50m on 1 September 2025. Money in payment apps and e-money wallets is usually not covered.

Inheritance Tax in South Korea

South Korea has estate tax. Children can receive ₩500 million lump-sum deduction (typical) tax-free, and the top rate for children is 50%, 2nd highest of 28 countries. Rates 10% to 50% above ₩3 billion; a move to taxing each heir's share is planned for 2028.
