Quick answer: A second-chance bank account is a checking account for people who've been turned down elsewhere because of a negative ChexSystems or Early Warning Services record, usually from an unpaid overdraft or an account closed by a bank. Your options fall into three groups:

  • Online accounts such as Chime and Varo, which are widely reported not to screen ChexSystems.
  • Checkless "safe" accounts at big banks with no overdrafts, such as Wells Fargo Clear Access Banking ($5 a month) and Chase Secure Banking ($4.95 a month).
  • "Second chance checking" at local credit unions, often $5 to $15 a month, which usually lets you move to a regular account after 6 to 12 months of good use.

Before applying, pull your free ChexSystems report and settle or dispute what's on it.

About 4% of US households, roughly 5.6 million, had no bank account in the FDIC's 2023 survey, and a bad banking record is one of the reasons people give. Without an account you pay to cash cheques, can't receive direct deposits easily, and pay more for almost everything. The good news is that a banking record clears faster than a credit score, and the way back is well defined.

Why Banks Turn People Down

When you apply for a checking account, most banks check a consumer reporting agency that tracks banking history. The main ones are ChexSystems and Early Warning Services. Negative records include:

  • An overdraft or negative balance that was never repaid.
  • An account closed by the bank "for cause".
  • Bounced cheques, or suspected fraud.
  • Many accounts opened in a short time.

ChexSystems keeps negative records for up to five years. Unlike a credit score, the record isn't a number. Each bank decides which records it will accept.

Step 1: Get Your Report and Clean It Up

  1. Request your free ChexSystems report at chexsystems.com. You're entitled to one free report every 12 months, and another after any denial. Early Warning Services also gives a free report on request.
  2. Check every entry. Errors, debts that aren't yours, and records past the five-year limit can be disputed with ChexSystems and the bank that reported them. They have 30 days to investigate.
  3. Pay what you genuinely owe. Contact the bank that reported the debt, pay it, and ask for written confirmation and for the record to be updated as paid. Some banks will open an account for applicants whose old debt shows as paid.
  4. Put a security freeze on your ChexSystems file if identity theft caused the record.

Online Accounts: The Fastest Route

Chime and Varo are financial technology companies whose accounts are held at FDIC-insured partner banks. They're widely reported to open accounts without checking ChexSystems, and they charge no monthly fee. They offer early direct deposit and fee-free ATMs within their networks.

Before relying on one as your main account, understand the drawbacks:

  • Cash deposits usually go through retailers and may cost a fee.
  • Customer service is mostly in the app.
  • Your money is protected by FDIC insurance only through the partner bank, and only once records correctly show the money is yours. The 2024 collapse of the middleware firm Synapse froze thousands of fintech customers' money for months.

Checkless Accounts at Big Banks

Accounts such as Wells Fargo Clear Access Banking and Chase Secure Banking come with a debit card but no paper cheques, and don't allow overdrafts. A purchase that would overdraw the account is declined instead. That lowers the bank's risk, which is why these accounts are often the easiest way back into a large bank.

The monthly fee, about $5, is usually waived for customers aged roughly 13 to 24 and sometimes with monthly electronic deposits. After a year of good use, ask about upgrading to a standard account with cheques.

Credit Union Second-Chance Checking

Many credit unions run accounts aimed at people with negative ChexSystems records. Some ask you to complete a short financial education course first. Typical features:

  • A monthly fee of $5 to $15.
  • Limits on cheque writing or none at all.
  • A "graduation" review after 6 to 12 months, after which you can move to regular checking.

To join a credit union you need to meet its membership rules, usually where you live or work, though many are open to almost anyone for a small donation to a partner charity.

How to Use the Account to Rebuild

  • Set up direct deposit so money arrives regularly and fees are often waived.
  • Turn on low-balance alerts in the app.
  • Never let the account go negative. If the account allows overdrafts at all, opt out.
  • Keep it open and in good standing for 12 months, then ask to upgrade, or apply for a standard account elsewhere.
  • Keep a small buffer, even $100, so a mis-timed charge doesn't undo your progress.

Frequently Asked Questions: Second-Chance Bank Accounts

What banks don't use ChexSystems?

Online accounts such as Chime and Varo are widely reported not to use ChexSystems when you open an account. Banks don't always say publicly which reporting agencies they use, and policies change, so check before applying.

How long does a ChexSystems record last?

Up to five years, unless it's removed sooner after a successful dispute or by the bank that reported it.

Can I open a bank account if I owe another bank money?

Often yes, with an online account or a second-chance account. But paying the old debt and getting it marked as paid widens your options considerably.

Do second-chance accounts affect my credit score?

No. Checking accounts aren't reported to the credit bureaus, and ChexSystems enquiries don't affect your credit score. An unpaid overdraft sent to a collection agency can appear on your credit report, though.

How much do second-chance accounts cost?

Usually $0 to $15 a month. Online accounts are often free, big-bank checkless accounts charge about $5, and credit union second-chance accounts $5 to $15.

Can I get a second-chance account after fraud?

That's the hardest case. Most second-chance programmes exclude records for suspected fraud. If the fraud was identity theft, file a report at IdentityTheft.gov and dispute the record.

Final Thoughts

A bad banking record isn't permanent, and it doesn't keep you out of banking. Get your ChexSystems report, pay or dispute what's on it, and open the cheapest account that will take you: a free online account if you need speed, or a checkless or credit union second-chance account if you want a path back to a full bank. A year of clean use is usually all it takes to upgrade.
