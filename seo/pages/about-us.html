Blog Digest publishes clear, in-depth guides to money, economics, careers, psychology and well-being, written for readers everywhere. Every guide aims to answer a real question properly: what is actually happening, why it matters to you, and what you can do about it, without hype, jargon or a sales pitch.

What We Cover

We focus on the questions people search for and struggle to get a straight answer to. Our main topics are:

  • Personal finance: saving, budgeting, debt, credit scores, insurance, emergency funds, buying a home or a car, and planning for retirement.
  • Investing: shares, funds, bonds, gold, IPOs and how to think about risk, explained for ordinary investors rather than professionals.
  • Economics: inflation, interest rates, GDP, bond yields, currencies and the economic news that changes what things cost and what your savings earn.
  • Cryptocurrency: Bitcoin, stablecoins, crypto tax and safety, with a sober view of both the opportunities and the risks.
  • Career and workplace: pay raises, interviews, layoffs, burnout, leadership and how work is changing with artificial intelligence.
  • Psychology, relationships and well-being: habits, stress, focus, confidence and the relationships that shape our lives.
  • Living abroad: moving countries, pensions and taxes across borders, and comparing life in different places.

Who We Write For

Blog Digest is written for an intelligent general reader in any country. Many money websites assume their readers live in one place and use one currency. We do not. Where rules differ between countries, as they do for taxes, pensions, deposit protection or credit scores, we explain the general principle first and then show how it works in the United States, the United Kingdom, the European Union, Canada, Australia, India and other major economies. Our calculators let you choose your currency, and they adjust to euros or pounds automatically for readers in Europe and the UK.

You do not need any background in finance or economics to read our guides. If you do have one, we hope you will still find something useful: we try to explain the reasoning behind each rule of thumb, not just repeat it.

How Our Guides Are Made

Every guide follows the same standards, which are set out in full in our editorial policy:

  • Researched from primary sources. Figures come from official and original sources wherever possible: central banks, national statistics offices, tax authorities, regulators and international organisations such as the IMF, the World Bank and the OECD. Each article links to its main sources in a "Sources" section, so you can check them yourself.
  • Dated and kept current. Each guide shows when it was last updated, and data is labelled with the month or year it refers to. When figures change materially, we update the article.
  • Quick answer first. Every guide opens with a short answer to the question in its title, followed by the full explanation for readers who want the detail.
  • Interactive tools. Most guides include a calculator or interactive diagram, so you can apply the idea to your own numbers rather than someone else's example.
  • Plain language. We explain technical terms when we first use them and avoid unnecessary jargon.

Our articles are researched and drafted with the help of AI tools and checked against the sources linked in each article before they are published. We do not invent experts, quotes or credentials, and every article is published under the name Blog Digest.

We do not sell financial products, and no article is written to push you towards a particular bank, broker, insurer or app. When we mention a company or product, it is because it helps explain the topic.

A Short History

Blog Digest began in 2021 as a personal blog of essays on life, work, money and the mind. Over time, the questions readers brought to it became more practical: how to manage debt, whether to invest in gold, how an election system works, what a recession means for an ordinary household. In 2026 we rebuilt the site around those questions, rewriting older articles to a much higher standard, adding calculators and interactive diagrams, and publishing new in-depth guides on the money and economic questions that matter most right now.
