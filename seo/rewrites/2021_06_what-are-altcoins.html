Quick answer: Altcoins are any cryptocurrencies other than Bitcoin. They include large platforms such as Ethereum and Solana, stablecoins pegged to currencies like the US dollar, exchange tokens, meme coins and thousands of smaller projects. Some aim to improve on Bitcoin's speed, cost or features; others power specific applications. Altcoins are generally riskier and more volatile than Bitcoin, and many have failed, so research carefully and only invest money you can afford to lose.

When Bitcoin launched in 2009, it was the only cryptocurrency. Today there are many thousands of others, collectively known as altcoins, short for "alternative coins". Some have become major technology platforms worth hundreds of billions of dollars at their peaks; others have disappeared within months.

This guide explains what altcoins are, the main types, why people use and invest in them, the risks involved and how to evaluate a project before buying.

What Is an Altcoin?

An altcoin is any cryptocurrency that isn't Bitcoin. Like Bitcoin, most altcoins run on a blockchain, a shared digital ledger maintained by a network of computers rather than a central bank or company. But altcoins differ in their purpose, technology, supply rules and how transactions are confirmed.

The first altcoins appeared around 2011. Namecoin and Litecoin were among the earliest, with Litecoin designed as a faster, lighter version of Bitcoin. The launch of Ethereum in 2015 transformed the space by allowing developers to build applications on a blockchain.

Main Types of Altcoins

Smart Contract Platforms

These blockchains let developers build decentralised applications (dApps) using self-executing programs called smart contracts. Ethereum is the largest. Others include Solana, Cardano, Avalanche and BNB Chain. Their native coins pay for transactions and computing on the network.

Stablecoins

Stablecoins aim to keep a steady value, usually pegged 1:1 to a currency such as the US dollar. Tether (USDT) and USD Coin (USDC) are the biggest. They are widely used for trading and payments, but they depend on the issuer actually holding adequate reserves. Some algorithmic stablecoins, notably TerraUSD in 2022, have collapsed.

Payment Coins

Coins such as Litecoin and Bitcoin Cash focus on faster or cheaper transactions than Bitcoin.

Exchange and Utility Tokens

Some tokens are issued by crypto exchanges or projects to provide discounts, access to services or other benefits within a specific platform.

Governance Tokens

These give holders voting rights over decisions in decentralised finance (DeFi) protocols and other projects.

Privacy Coins

Monero and Zcash use advanced cryptography to hide transaction details. Because of regulatory concerns, some exchanges in various countries have delisted them.

Meme Coins

Dogecoin began as a joke in 2013, and many similar "meme coins" have followed. Their prices are driven mainly by social media hype and community enthusiasm rather than technology, making them extremely volatile.

Why Altcoins Became Popular

  • New uses: smart contracts enabled DeFi lending and trading, NFTs, blockchain gaming and tokenised assets.
  • Speed and cost: many networks process transactions faster and more cheaply than Bitcoin's base layer.
  • Energy use: proof-of-stake networks use far less energy than mining-based systems. Ethereum switched to proof of stake in 2022.
  • Potential returns: smaller coins can rise sharply, attracting speculators.
  • Easy access: exchanges and apps made it simple for anyone to buy small amounts.

Are Altcoins Safe? The Main Risks

  • Extreme volatility: prices can fall 80% to 90% or more in bear markets, and many never recover.
  • Project failure: a large share of coins launched in past boom years are now inactive or near worthless.
  • Scams and fraud: "rug pulls", where developers abandon a project and take investors' money, and pump-and-dump schemes are common.
  • Low liquidity: small coins can be hard to sell without moving the price.
  • Technical risk: bugs in smart contracts and hacks of bridges or exchanges have caused large losses.
  • Regulatory risk: governments are still developing crypto rules. Some tokens may be treated as securities, and taxes on gains apply in many countries.
  • Concentration: founders or early investors may hold large shares and sell at any time.

How to Evaluate an Altcoin Before Investing

  1. Purpose: what real problem does it solve, and does it need a token to do so?
  2. Team: are the developers public, experienced and credible?
  3. Usage: are people actually using the network, or is activity mainly speculation?
  4. Tokenomics: what is the total supply, how is it distributed and when do locked tokens unlock?
  5. Security: has the code been independently audited? Has it been hacked before?
  6. Community and development: is the code actively maintained?
  7. Listing and liquidity: is it traded on reputable exchanges with meaningful volume?

Practical Tips If You Decide to Invest

  • Only invest money you can afford to lose entirely.
  • Keep crypto as a small part of a diversified portfolio.
  • Use reputable, regulated exchanges and enable two-factor authentication.
  • Consider a hardware wallet for long-term holdings, and never share your seed phrase.
  • Be wary of anyone promising guaranteed returns, and of "influencer" tips.
  • Keep records for tax purposes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Ethereum an altcoin?

Yes. Technically, any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin is an altcoin, and Ethereum is the largest by market value.

How many altcoins are there?

Many thousands, and new tokens are created every day. Only a relatively small number have significant trading volume and lasting use.

Can altcoins overtake Bitcoin?

It has never happened so far by market value. Some altcoins may grow in importance for specific uses, but predicting which ones is very difficult.

What is "altcoin season"?

It's an informal term for periods when altcoins rise faster than Bitcoin, often late in crypto bull markets. These periods have historically been followed by sharp declines.

Final Thoughts

Altcoins have pushed blockchain technology far beyond Bitcoin, creating platforms for finance, applications and digital ownership. They have also produced some of the biggest speculative bubbles and scams in modern finance. If you're curious, learn how a project works, understand its risks and keep your investment small. In crypto, curiosity is healthy, but caution protects your money.
