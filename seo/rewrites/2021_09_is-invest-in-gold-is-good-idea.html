Quick answer: Investing in gold can be a good idea as part of a diversified portfolio, but not as your only investment. Gold tends to hold its value over long periods, often rises during crises, and can protect against currency weakness and inflation over the long run. However, it pays no interest or dividends, its price can stay flat or fall for years, and physical gold has storage and making costs. Many financial planners suggest keeping around 5% to 15% of a portfolio in gold, preferably through low-cost options such as gold ETFs or gold mutual funds rather than jewellery.

Gold has fascinated people for thousands of years. It has been used as money, as a symbol of wealth and status, and as a way to protect savings during wars, inflation and political turmoil. In India, gold also has deep cultural significance, from weddings to festivals like Akshaya Tritiya and Dhanteras. Indian households are believed to hold one of the largest private stocks of gold in the world.

But is gold actually a good investment today? This guide looks at gold's strengths and weaknesses, how it has performed, the different ways to invest, and how much of your money it might make sense to keep in gold.

Why People Invest in Gold

1. A Long-Term Store of Value

Gold is scarce, durable and can't be printed by governments. Over long periods it has tended to hold its purchasing power, while paper currencies have steadily lost value to inflation. The rupee, for example, has weakened significantly against the US dollar over the decades, and gold priced in rupees has benefited from that.

2. A Safe Haven in Crises

When stock markets fall sharply or the world faces uncertainty, investors often move money into gold. During the 2008 global financial crisis and the early months of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, gold held up far better than shares over the following year and reached new highs.

3. Diversification

Gold's price often moves differently from shares and bonds. Adding a modest amount of gold to a portfolio can reduce overall swings in value, because gold may rise when other assets fall.

4. Hedge Against Currency Weakness

Gold is priced internationally in US dollars. For Indian investors, a weaker rupee makes gold more expensive in rupee terms, so gold can partly protect your wealth if the rupee falls.

5. Liquidity

Gold is easy to buy and sell. Gold ETFs can be traded on stock exchanges during market hours, and physical gold can be sold to jewellers or pledged for a loan almost anywhere.

6. Central Bank Demand

In recent years, central banks around the world, including the Reserve Bank of India, have been buying large amounts of gold to diversify their reserves. This steady demand has supported prices and is one reason gold reached record highs in 2024 and 2025.

The Downsides of Gold

1. No Income

Gold doesn't pay interest, dividends or rent. Your return depends entirely on the price rising. Shares grow with company profits, and fixed deposits pay interest, but a gold bar today is the same gold bar in 20 years.

2. Long Periods of Poor Returns

Gold can go through long flat or falling periods. After peaking in 2011, international gold prices fell substantially and took several years to recover. Investors who bought at the peak waited many years to break even in dollar terms.

3. Volatility

Although considered "safe", gold can swing sharply in the short term. It is less volatile than many individual shares but more volatile than fixed deposits or high-quality bonds.

4. Costs of Physical Gold

Jewellery comes with making charges, often 8% to 25% of the gold value, which you usually don't recover when selling. Coins and bars carry smaller premiums but still involve storage costs, bank locker fees, insurance and the risk of theft. Purity can also be an issue if you don't buy hallmarked gold.

5. Taxes

Gains on gold are taxable. In India, the tax treatment depends on the form of gold and how long you hold it, and the rules were changed significantly in 2024. Check the current rules before investing.

How Has Gold Performed?

Over the very long term, gold has roughly kept pace with or beaten inflation, and in rupee terms it has delivered strong returns thanks partly to the weakening rupee. Over shorter periods, its returns have varied widely:

  • 2001 to 2011: a powerful bull market, with gold rising many times over in dollar terms.
  • 2011 to 2015: a major decline after the peak, as the global economy recovered and interest rates were expected to rise.
  • 2019 to 2020: strong gains as interest rates fell and the pandemic hit.
  • 2024 to 2025: a dramatic rally to record highs, with international prices going above 4,000 US dollars per ounce for the first time in October 2025, driven by central bank buying, geopolitical tensions and expectations of lower interest rates.

Over long periods, broad stock market indices have generally delivered higher returns than gold, because companies grow and pay dividends. But gold has often done well exactly when shares have done badly, which is what makes it useful in a portfolio.

What Drives the Price of Gold?

  • Interest rates: when real interest rates (rates after inflation) are low or falling, gold tends to do well, because the opportunity cost of holding a non-interest-paying asset is lower.
  • The US dollar: a weaker dollar usually pushes gold prices up in dollar terms.
  • Inflation expectations: fears of high inflation can increase demand for gold.
  • Geopolitical risk: wars, sanctions and political crises often boost demand.
  • Central bank buying: large purchases by central banks support prices.
  • Jewellery demand: especially from India and China, which can rise or fall with prices and incomes.
  • Investor sentiment: flows into gold ETFs and futures markets can move prices significantly.

Ways to Invest in Gold

1. Physical Gold: Jewellery, Coins and Bars

The traditional route. Jewellery has emotional and practical value, but high making charges make it a poor pure investment. Coins and bars from banks or reputable jewellers are better for investment, but still carry premiums and storage costs. Always buy BIS-hallmarked gold with a Hallmark Unique Identification (HUID) number to ensure purity.

2. Gold ETFs

Gold exchange-traded funds hold physical gold of high purity and trade on stock exchanges like shares. You need a demat and trading account. Advantages include low costs, no storage worries, high liquidity and prices closely linked to the actual gold price. Expense ratios are typically well under 1% a year.

3. Gold Mutual Funds

Gold fund-of-funds invest in gold ETFs and don't require a demat account. You can invest through a SIP with small monthly amounts. They're slightly more expensive than ETFs because of the extra layer of fees, but they're convenient for regular investing.

4. Sovereign Gold Bonds (SGBs)

SGBs were government securities issued by the RBI, linked to the gold price and paying 2.5% annual interest on the initial investment. Gains held to maturity were tax-free. They were popular, but the government has stopped issuing new tranches. Existing bonds can still be bought and sold on stock exchanges, sometimes at a premium or discount to the gold price, and existing holders continue to receive interest until redemption.

5. Digital Gold

Many apps let you buy gold in very small amounts, with the provider storing the physical gold for you. It's convenient, but digital gold is not regulated by SEBI or the RBI, which means investor protection is limited. SEBI has warned investors about these products. If you use digital gold, keep amounts small and use well-known providers.

6. Gold Mining Shares and Funds

Shares of gold mining companies tend to rise more than gold when prices rise, and fall more when prices fall. They also carry company-specific risks such as costs, management and politics in mining regions. They're a more aggressive way to gain exposure.

How Much Gold Should You Own?

There's no single correct answer, but a common guideline is to keep around 5% to 15% of your investment portfolio in gold. Factors to consider:

  • Your other investments: if you hold mostly shares, a slightly higher gold allocation adds more diversification.
  • Existing jewellery: many Indian households already own a lot of gold in jewellery. Count it, at least partly, before buying more.
  • Your goals: gold is better for preserving wealth and reducing risk than for maximising long-term growth.
  • Your time horizon: gold can be flat for years, so it's best held for the long term.

Once you've chosen an allocation, rebalance once a year. If gold has risen and now makes up much more than your target, sell some and invest in other assets; if it has fallen, top it up. This disciplined approach naturally buys low and sells high.

Tax on Gold Investments in India

Tax rules for gold were simplified in the 2024 Budget. In broad terms:

  • Physical gold and digital gold: gains on assets held for more than 24 months are treated as long-term and taxed at 12.5% without indexation. Shorter-term gains are added to your income and taxed at your slab rate.
  • Gold ETFs: long-term if held for more than 12 months, taxed at 12.5%. Short-term gains are taxed at slab rates.
  • Gold mutual funds (fund of funds): long-term if held for more than 24 months, taxed at 12.5%, with shorter-term gains taxed at slab rates.
  • Sovereign Gold Bonds: interest is taxable as income, but capital gains on redemption at maturity are tax-free for individuals. Gains on selling SGBs on the exchange before maturity are taxed as capital gains.
  • GST: 3% GST applies to the purchase of physical and digital gold, plus GST on making charges for jewellery.

Tax rules can change in any Budget, so confirm the latest position or speak to a tax adviser before buying or selling.

Gold's Role in Indian Culture and Household Finance

For many Indian families, gold is far more than an investment. It's gifted at weddings and births, bought on auspicious days, and passed down through generations. For women in particular, gold jewellery has traditionally provided a form of financial security that is personally owned.

This cultural role is valuable, and there's nothing wrong with buying jewellery you'll wear and treasure. The key is to separate two goals in your mind: gold you buy to use and keep, and gold you hold purely as an investment. For the second, low-cost financial forms of gold are almost always the better choice, because more of your money goes into gold itself rather than into making charges and margins.

Families can also make better use of jewellery they already own. Keeping a record of each item's weight and purity, storing it securely, and knowing that it can be used as collateral for a low-cost gold loan in an emergency all turn idle gold into a more useful part of household finances.

How to Check Gold Purity

Buying genuine gold is essential, especially for jewellery, coins and bars. In India:

  • Look for the BIS hallmark: hallmarking is mandatory for gold jewellery and artefacts in most districts. The hallmark includes the BIS logo, the purity grade (such as 22K916 for 22-carat gold) and a six-digit HUID code.
  • Verify the HUID: you can check the HUID number using the BIS CARE app to confirm the item's details.
  • Understand carats: 24-carat gold is about 99.9% pure and is used for coins and bars. Jewellery is usually 22 or 18 carat, because pure gold is too soft for everyday wear.
  • Get a proper invoice: it should show the weight, purity, gold rate, making charges and GST separately.

A Simple Example: SIP in a Gold Fund

Suppose Kavya invests 3,000 rupees a month in a gold mutual fund through a SIP. When gold prices are high, she buys fewer units; when prices dip, the same 3,000 rupees buys more. Over several years, she builds a meaningful gold holding without worrying about timing, storage or purity, and without paying making charges. Each year she checks that gold makes up about 10% of her total investments and adjusts her SIP if needed. It's a quiet, disciplined approach that suits most investors far better than occasional large purchases driven by headlines.

Gold and Silver: Should You Own Both?

Silver is often grouped with gold as a precious metal, and silver ETFs are now available in India. Silver has both investment and industrial demand, from electronics to solar panels, which makes it more volatile than gold. It can rise faster in bull markets but also fall harder. For most investors, gold should be the main precious metal holding, with silver at most a small additional allocation for those comfortable with bigger price swings.

Common Mistakes When Investing in Gold

  • Treating jewellery as investment. Making charges and resale discounts can wipe out years of price gains.
  • Buying after big price spikes because of fear of missing out.
  • Putting too much in gold and missing the long-term growth of shares.
  • Ignoring purity by buying non-hallmarked gold.
  • Using unregulated platforms for large digital gold purchases.
  • Not rebalancing, letting gold become far larger or smaller than planned.

Smart Ways to Add Gold to Your Portfolio

  1. Decide on a target percentage, such as 10% of your investments.
  2. Choose a low-cost vehicle, such as a gold ETF or gold mutual fund.
  3. Build your position gradually through monthly SIPs instead of buying all at once.
  4. Hold for the long term, ideally five years or more.
  5. Rebalance annually back to your target.
  6. Buy jewellery for its personal and cultural value, not as your main investment.

Gold Loans: Using Gold You Already Own

If your family already owns gold jewellery, it can serve as a useful financial backup. Banks and NBFCs offer gold loans quickly, often within hours, at interest rates lower than personal loans or credit cards. The RBI sets limits on how much can be lent against gold. Gold loans are best used for short-term needs; if you can't repay, the lender can auction the gold.

Frequently Asked Questions: Is Investing in Gold a Good Idea

Is it worth investing $10,000 in gold?

It can be, as one part of a diversified portfolio. Many advisers suggest keeping gold to about 5–10% of your investments: it tends to hold value when stocks and currencies fall, but it pays no interest or dividends and can drop sharply, as it did in 2026. Low-cost gold ETFs or sovereign gold bonds avoid the storage and making charges of physical gold.

Is gold a good investment right now?

Nobody can reliably predict short-term gold prices. After a big rally, prices can pause or fall. Rather than trying to time the market, invest gradually towards a target allocation and hold for the long term.

Is gold better than stocks?

Over the long term, stocks have generally delivered higher returns because companies grow and pay dividends. Gold's main value is diversification and protection during crises. Most investors benefit from owning both.

Does gold protect against inflation?

Over long periods, gold has tended to keep up with inflation, but over shorter periods its link with inflation is inconsistent. It's better seen as a long-term store of value than a precise inflation hedge.

Which is better: gold ETF or physical gold?

For investment purposes, gold ETFs are usually better: lower costs, no storage or purity concerns, and easy buying and selling. Physical gold makes sense if you value holding it in hand or need jewellery.

Can I start investing in gold with a small amount?

Yes. Gold mutual funds allow SIPs from small monthly amounts, and you can buy a single unit of a gold ETF, which usually represents a small fraction of a gram.

What is the best time to buy gold in India?

Many people buy around festivals and weddings for cultural reasons, but for investment purposes, regular buying through a SIP removes the need to pick a perfect time.

Is digital gold safe?

Digital gold from well-known providers is usually backed by physical gold held with a custodian, but it falls outside SEBI and RBI regulation, so you have fewer protections if something goes wrong. Gold ETFs and gold mutual funds are regulated alternatives.

Should I sell gold when prices are at a record high?

Selling everything because of a high price is a form of market timing. A better approach is to rebalance: if gold has grown well beyond your target share of your portfolio, sell the excess and reinvest it elsewhere.

Can NRIs invest in gold in India?

Non-resident Indians can generally invest in gold ETFs and gold mutual funds in India through their NRE or NRO accounts, subject to the fund house's rules. Check the specific fund's eligibility and tax implications in your country of residence.

How do I sell physical gold for the best price?

Compare offers from a few reputable jewellers or gold buyers, ask how purity will be tested, and check whether any deductions apply. Keep original invoices, as they help establish purity and purchase cost for tax purposes.

Is gold a good gift for children's future?

Gold can be a meaningful gift, but for long-term goals such as education, a combination of equity funds and a small gold fund holding is usually more effective than jewellery alone.

Final Thoughts

Gold is neither a magic wealth-builder nor a relic to be ignored. It's a useful supporting player in a portfolio: a store of value, a diversifier and a cushion during crises. Keep it to a sensible share of your investments, choose low-cost forms such as ETFs and gold funds, and let assets like equities do the heavy lifting for long-term growth. Treat jewellery as something to enjoy, not as your retirement plan.
