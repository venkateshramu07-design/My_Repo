Quick answer: You can tell someone really loves you by how they act consistently over time, not by grand gestures or words alone. Genuine love shows up as acceptance of who you are, attentive listening, reliability, everyday acts of care, honest communication, respect for your boundaries, support for your growth and a willingness to work through conflict. If someone's actions match their words, especially when things are difficult, that is one of the clearest signs their love is real.

"I love you" are three of the most powerful words in any language, but they are also easy to say. Most of us have, at some point, wondered whether someone's feelings are as deep as they claim. Is this real love, or attraction, habit, convenience or fear of being alone?

There is no test that gives a certain answer, but psychologists and relationship researchers agree on a set of behaviours that consistently show up in healthy, loving relationships. This guide covers those signs, the red flags that suggest something else is going on, and how to talk about your doubts.

Why Actions Matter More Than Words

Words express feelings in the moment, while actions reveal priorities over time. Someone who truly loves you will show it in small, repeated choices: making time for you, remembering what matters to you and treating you with care when they are tired, stressed or upset. Look at the overall pattern rather than one perfect date or one bad day.

1. They Accept You as You Are

Real love doesn't come with a long list of conditions. A partner who loves you appreciates your strengths and accepts your flaws, quirks and history without constantly trying to change who you fundamentally are.

  • You feel safe being yourself, including on bad days.
  • They encourage healthy changes you want to make, but don't demand that you become someone else.
  • They don't make you feel ashamed of your past, your family or your interests.

2. They Truly Listen to You

Active listening is one of the clearest everyday signs of love. Someone who cares about you wants to understand your inner world.

  • They put the phone down and give you their attention when you talk.
  • They remember details: the name of your difficult coworker, the date of your exam, your favourite food.
  • They ask follow-up questions and validate your feelings, even when they see things differently.

3. They Are Consistent and Reliable

Love that comes and goes depending on mood, convenience or who else is around is not secure love. Consistency builds trust.

  • They do what they say they will do.
  • Their behaviour toward you is similar in private and in public.
  • You don't have to guess whether they will be warm or cold from one day to the next.

4. They Show Care Through Everyday Actions

Love is often quiet. It looks like making you tea when you're tired, checking that you got home safely, helping with chores or showing up when you're sick. Psychologist Gary Chapman's idea of "love languages" suggests people express love in different ways, such as words of affirmation, quality time, acts of service, gifts and physical touch. Your partner may show love differently from how you do, so notice the ways they try.

5. They Communicate Openly and Honestly

People who love you don't keep you guessing. They are honest about their feelings, plans and concerns, even when the conversation is uncomfortable.

  • They share what's going on in their lives.
  • They tell you when something bothers them rather than going silent or punishing you.
  • They don't lie about where they are or who they are with.

6. They Respect You and Your Boundaries

Respect is the foundation of love. A loving partner:

  • Accepts "no" without pressuring, sulking or punishing.
  • Values your opinions and includes you in decisions that affect you.
  • Respects your time, privacy, friendships and independence.
  • Never belittles you, especially in front of others.

7. They Support Your Growth and Dreams

When someone loves you, your happiness and success matter to them. They celebrate your achievements, encourage your goals and support you through setbacks, instead of feeling threatened or competing with you.

8. They Include You in Their Life and Future

People in love naturally want to share their life with their partner. Over time, they introduce you to friends and family, make plans with you and talk about a shared future. If after a long time you are still kept separate from their world, it is worth asking why.

9. They Work Through Conflict With You

All couples disagree. What matters is how they handle it. Someone who loves you wants to resolve problems, not win arguments.

  • They can apologise and take responsibility.
  • They fight fair, without insults, threats or bringing up old wounds to hurt you.
  • They are willing to compromise and repair the relationship after an argument.

10. They Are There When It's Hard

It is easy to love someone when life is fun. Real love shows up during illness, job loss, grief and stress. A partner who stays kind and present during your hardest times is giving you one of the strongest signs of genuine love.

Red Flags That Suggest It May Not Be Real Love

  • Love bombing: overwhelming affection, gifts and promises very early, followed by withdrawal or control.
  • Control and jealousy: monitoring your phone, limiting who you see or deciding what you wear.
  • Hot and cold behaviour: affection only when it suits them.
  • Constant criticism or contempt: making you feel small, stupid or unworthy.
  • Words without actions: repeated promises that never materialise.
  • Disrespecting boundaries: ignoring your "no", whether emotional, physical or sexual.

Any form of physical violence, threats or coercion is abuse, not love. If you feel unsafe, contact a local domestic violence helpline or emergency services.

How to Talk About Your Doubts

  1. Clarify what you need. Is it more time together, more reassurance or more commitment?
  2. Choose a calm moment. Avoid raising big questions during an argument.
  3. Use "I" statements. "I feel unsure when plans change at the last minute" invites conversation; "You never care" invites defence.
  4. Listen to their answer. They may show love in ways you haven't recognised.
  5. Watch what happens next. Loving partners respond to your needs with changed behaviour, not just reassurance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to know if someone loves you?

Strong feelings can appear quickly, but deep, reliable love becomes clear over months, as you see how someone behaves in different situations, including stressful ones.

Can someone love you but not show it?

People express love differently, and some find it hard to show emotion. However, love that is never shown in any way leaves you feeling unloved, which is worth discussing openly.

What is the difference between love and infatuation?

Infatuation is intense, focused on excitement and often on an idealised image of the person. Love is calmer and deeper, and accepts the real person, flaws included.

Is jealousy a sign of love?

Occasional mild jealousy is human, but possessive or controlling jealousy is a sign of insecurity or control, not love.

What if I'm not sure I love them?

That's normal, especially early on. Notice whether you feel safe, respected and happier with them. Love grows with time, trust and shared experiences.

Final Thoughts

Real love is less about fireworks and more about steady warmth. It shows in how someone listens, keeps their promises, respects your boundaries, supports your dreams and stays beside you when life is hard. Trust what you see over time, talk honestly about what you need, and remember that you deserve a love that makes you feel safe, valued and free to be yourself.
