Quick answer: Joe Biden served as the 46th President of the United States from 2021 to 2025 and as Vice President under Barack Obama from 2009 to 2017. Lesser-known facts about him include his lifelong struggle with a stutter, his election to the US Senate at just 29, the car crash that killed his first wife and daughter weeks later, his decades of daily train commuting between Delaware and Washington, and his record as the oldest person ever to serve as US President.

Joe Biden spent more than half a century in American public life, yet many people know him only through headlines. Behind the political career is a personal story shaped by childhood challenges, family tragedy and an unusually long path to the presidency.

Here are some lesser-known facts about his life and career.

Quick Profile

  • Full name: Joseph Robinette Biden Jr.
  • Born: 20 November 1942, Scranton, Pennsylvania
  • Party: Democratic
  • US Senator for Delaware: 1973 to 2009
  • Vice President: 2009 to 2017, under Barack Obama
  • President: 20 January 2021 to 20 January 2025
  • Spouse: Jill Biden (married 1977); first wife Neilia Hunter Biden died in 1972

1. He Overcame a Childhood Stutter

Biden stuttered as a child and was teased by classmates, who nicknamed him "Dash" after the Morse code dash. He has described practising by reciting poetry by Yeats and Emerson in front of a mirror to control his speech. As an adult he has mentored young people who stutter and spoken openly about the experience.

2. His Family Moved to Delaware When He Was Young

Biden was born in Scranton, Pennsylvania, but his family moved to Delaware when he was about 10 after his father struggled to find work. His father eventually found steady employment as a car salesman. Biden often credited his father's line, "Champ, when you get knocked down, get up", as a guiding principle.

3. He Was Elected to the Senate at 29

In 1972 Biden ran for the US Senate in Delaware as a long-shot candidate and narrowly defeated a well-known incumbent. He was 29 on election day and turned 30, the constitutional minimum age for a senator, just before taking office. That made him one of the youngest people ever elected to the Senate.

4. Tragedy Struck Weeks After His First Election

In December 1972, just weeks after his election, his wife Neilia and their one-year-old daughter Naomi were killed in a car accident while Christmas shopping. His young sons, Beau and Hunter, were badly injured. Biden was sworn in to the Senate at his sons' hospital bedside.

5. He Commuted by Train for Decades

To be home with his sons each night, Biden commuted roughly 90 minutes each way by Amtrak between Wilmington and Washington throughout his Senate career. He became so associated with the line that he earned the nickname "Amtrak Joe", and Wilmington's station was later renamed the Joseph R. Biden Jr. Railroad Station.

6. He Survived Two Brain Aneurysms

In 1988 Biden suffered two brain aneurysms that required surgery. He was away from the Senate for several months while recovering, then returned to work.

7. He Ran for President Three Times

Biden first sought the presidency in 1988 and again in 2008, withdrawing early both times. His third campaign, in 2020, succeeded when he defeated incumbent Donald Trump.

8. He Lost His Son Beau to Cancer

Biden's eldest son, Beau, a former Attorney General of Delaware and Iraq War veteran, died of brain cancer in 2015 at age 46. Biden later wrote about the experience in his memoir Promise Me, Dad and, as Vice President, led the "Cancer Moonshot" initiative to speed up cancer research.

9. He Chaired Major Senate Committees

During his 36 years in the Senate, Biden chaired the Senate Judiciary Committee and the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. He was a principal author of the 1994 Violence Against Women Act, which he often called one of his proudest achievements.

10. He Received the Presidential Medal of Freedom as Vice President

In January 2017, President Obama surprised Biden by awarding him the Presidential Medal of Freedom with distinction, the nation's highest civilian honour, in a ceremony at the White House.

11. He Was the Oldest Serving President in US History

Biden was 78 when he took office in 2021, making him the oldest person ever inaugurated as US President at that time, and he was 82 when he left office. He initially ran for re-election in 2024 but withdrew in July 2024 and endorsed Vice President Kamala Harris as the Democratic nominee.

12. His Wife Kept Teaching While First Lady

Jill Biden, who holds a doctorate in education, continued teaching English at a community college while serving as First Lady, becoming the first First Lady to hold a paid job outside the White House during her husband's presidency.

13. He Is a Practising Catholic

Biden was the second Catholic President of the United States, after John F. Kennedy. He frequently spoke about how his faith helped him through personal losses.

14. He Studied History and Political Science, Then Law

Biden graduated from the University of Delaware in 1965 with a double major in history and political science. He then earned a law degree from Syracuse University College of Law in 1968. He has openly acknowledged that he was not a top student at either school; he later said he found his real motivation once he entered public life. After law school he worked briefly as a lawyer, including as a public defender, before entering local politics.

15. He Started on a County Council

Before reaching the Senate, Biden served on the New Castle County Council in Delaware from 1970 to 1972. His experience in local government, dealing with issues like zoning and highways, was the springboard for his surprise Senate run two years later.

16. He Met Jill Biden on a Blind Date

Several years after his first wife's death, Biden's brother Frank set him up on a blind date with Jill Jacobs, a college student and former model whose photo Biden had seen in an advertisement. Jill reportedly turned down his marriage proposal several times before agreeing. They married in 1977 at the United Nations chapel in New York, and their daughter Ashley was born in 1981.

17. He Never Became Wealthy in the Senate

For most of his Senate career, Biden was often listed among the least wealthy members of Congress. He frequently mentioned living on his government salary and joked about it. His finances changed significantly after his vice presidency through book deals and speaking engagements.

18. He Was Known for Foreign Policy Expertise

As a senator and chair of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, Biden travelled widely and built relationships with world leaders over decades. He was involved in debates over the Balkans in the 1990s, the Iraq War and arms control agreements. As Vice President, he handled major foreign policy assignments, including oversight of US policy in Iraq.

19. He Played a Key Role in the 2009 Recovery Act

After the 2008 financial crisis, President Obama put Vice President Biden in charge of overseeing the implementation of the American Recovery and Reinvestment Act, a stimulus package of roughly $800 billion. Biden's job was to monitor spending and prevent waste and fraud, which gave him extensive experience with large-scale government programmes.

20. He Loves Ice Cream and Aviator Sunglasses

On a lighter note, Biden became known for his love of ice cream, often visiting ice cream shops during campaign stops, and for his signature aviator sunglasses. Both became part of his public image and frequent subjects of photos and memes.

21. He Is a Lifelong Fan of Trains and Cars

Beyond Amtrak, Biden has a well-known fondness for cars, especially his 1967 Chevrolet Corvette Stingray, a wedding gift from his father. He has shown the car in videos and spoken about it often. As President, he promoted investment in rail and electric vehicles.

22. He Delivered Eulogies for Many Public Figures

Because of his long career and personal experience of loss, Biden was frequently asked to speak at memorial services. He became known for speeches about grief that drew on his own tragedies, offering comfort to families who had lost loved ones, including families of service members.

Growing Up Biden: Early Life and Family

Joseph Robinette Biden Jr. was the eldest of four children born to Joseph Biden Sr. and Catherine Eugenia "Jean" Finnegan Biden. The family was Irish Catholic and close-knit. His siblings, Valerie, Jimmy and Frank, remained central to his life; his sister Valerie Biden Owens managed many of his campaigns, starting with his first Senate race.

The family moved from Scranton to Claymont, Delaware, and later to Wilmington, when Biden was a child. He attended Archmere Academy, a Catholic preparatory school, where he played football and was elected class president. To help pay for school, he worked on the school grounds during summers.

Biden often spoke about the values his parents taught him: dignity, the importance of work, standing up to bullies and loyalty to family. His mother reportedly told him, "Nobody is better than you, and you are better than nobody," a line he quoted throughout his career.

Lessons in Resilience From Biden's Life

Whatever one's political views, Biden's personal story offers lessons about coping with adversity:

  • Grief can coexist with purpose. After losing his wife and daughter, and later his son, Biden continued working, often saying that purpose helped him survive grief.
  • Setbacks are not the end. He lost two presidential bids, faced health crises and endured public criticism, yet kept going.
  • Family support matters. His siblings and children played major roles in his recovery from tragedy and in his career.
  • Speaking openly helps others. His willingness to talk about stuttering and grief encouraged many people facing similar struggles.

Biden's Books

  • Promises to Keep: On Life and Politics (2007): a memoir covering his early life, family tragedies and Senate career.
  • Promise Me, Dad: A Year of Hope, Hardship, and Purpose (2017): about the year of his son Beau's illness and death, and his decision not to run for President in 2016.

Key Moments of the Biden Presidency

  • Leading the US response during the later stages of the COVID-19 pandemic, including a large-scale vaccination campaign.
  • Signing the American Rescue Plan, the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, the CHIPS and Science Act and the Inflation Reduction Act.
  • Overseeing the US withdrawal from Afghanistan in 2021.
  • Supporting Ukraine following Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022.
  • Appointing Ketanji Brown Jackson, the first Black woman on the US Supreme Court.

The Biden Presidency in More Detail

Domestic Policy

Biden's domestic agenda focused on pandemic recovery, infrastructure and industrial policy.

  • American Rescue Plan (2021): a $1.9 trillion COVID-19 relief package that included direct payments to many Americans, expanded child tax credits for a year, funding for vaccines and support for state and local governments.
  • Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (2021): a bipartisan law funding roads, bridges, public transit, broadband internet, water systems and electric vehicle charging.
  • CHIPS and Science Act (2022): provided subsidies and incentives to boost semiconductor manufacturing and research in the United States.
  • Inflation Reduction Act (2022): included major investments in clean energy, allowed Medicare to negotiate prices for some prescription drugs and capped insulin costs for Medicare recipients.
  • Bipartisan Safer Communities Act (2022): the most significant federal gun safety legislation in decades, expanding background checks for younger buyers and funding mental health programmes.
  • Respect for Marriage Act (2022): required federal recognition of same-sex and interracial marriages.

Challenges and Criticism

Like every President, Biden faced significant criticism:

  • Inflation: US inflation reached around 9% in mid-2022, the highest in four decades. Critics blamed stimulus spending, while supporters pointed to global supply chain and energy shocks.
  • Afghanistan: the chaotic US withdrawal in August 2021, including a bombing at Kabul airport that killed 13 US service members and many Afghans, was widely criticised.
  • Immigration: record numbers of encounters at the southern border during parts of his term became a major political issue.
  • Age: concerns about his age and stamina grew during his term and became central after a widely watched debate performance in June 2024, leading to his withdrawal from the race.

Foreign Policy

  • Rejoined the Paris climate agreement and the World Health Organization on his first day in office.
  • Rallied NATO allies to support Ukraine with weapons, aid and sanctions against Russia after February 2022.
  • Saw Finland and Sweden join NATO.
  • Strengthened alliances in the Indo-Pacific, including the Quad partnership with India, Japan and Australia and the AUKUS security pact with the UK and Australia.
  • Responded to the Hamas attack on Israel in October 2023 and the war in Gaza that followed, a policy that drew strong criticism from different directions.

A Senate Career Built on Relationships

Biden's 36 years in the Senate shaped his reputation as a dealmaker. He was known for building personal relationships across party lines, including with conservative Republicans, and he often spoke of the Senate's tradition of compromise. Some of his notable legislative work included:

  • The Violence Against Women Act (1994), which funded programmes to combat domestic violence and sexual assault.
  • The 1994 crime bill, which he helped write. It included the Violence Against Women Act and an assault weapons ban, but its tougher sentencing provisions were later criticised for contributing to mass incarceration, something Biden himself acknowledged in later years.
  • Major arms control debates, including support for treaties limiting nuclear weapons.
  • Oversight of Supreme Court confirmation hearings as Judiciary Committee chair, including the controversial 1991 hearings of Clarence Thomas and Anita Hill, which he later said he regretted handling as he did.

His long career meant that he had a record on nearly every major issue of the late 20th century, which both helped and challenged him as a presidential candidate.

Biden and India

Biden had a long record of engagement with India. As chair of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, he supported the 2008 US–India civil nuclear agreement, a landmark in relations between the two countries. As Vice President, he visited India in 2013 and spoke about the potential of the partnership. As President, he hosted Prime Minister Narendra Modi for a state visit in June 2023, during which the two governments announced cooperation in defence, semiconductors, space and critical technologies. He also attended the G20 summit in New Delhi in September 2023 and deepened the Quad partnership between the US, India, Japan and Australia.

Memorable Quotes by Joe Biden

  • "Don't tell me what you value. Show me your budget, and I'll tell you what you value."
  • "Failure at some point in your life is inevitable, but giving up is unforgivable."
  • "Democracy is precious. Democracy is fragile." (from his 2021 inaugural address)

Life After the Presidency

Biden left office on 20 January 2025, at age 82, the oldest President to complete a term. He returned to Delaware. In May 2025, his office announced that he had been diagnosed with an aggressive form of prostate cancer that had spread to the bone, and that he and his family were reviewing treatment options with his doctors.

Frequently Asked Questions

How old is Joe Biden?

Joe Biden was born on 20 November 1942.

How many children does Joe Biden have?

He had four children: Beau, Hunter and Naomi with his first wife Neilia, and Ashley with Jill Biden. Naomi died in 1972 and Beau in 2015.

Which states is Joe Biden associated with?

He was born in Pennsylvania but built his entire political career in Delaware, which he represented in the Senate for 36 years.

Where did Joe Biden go to college?

He graduated from the University of Delaware in 1965 and earned his law degree from Syracuse University in 1968.

Who was Joe Biden's Vice President?

Kamala Harris, the first woman, first Black American and first South Asian American to serve as Vice President.

How long was Joe Biden in the Senate?

Thirty-six years, from January 1973 until January 2009, when he resigned to become Vice President.

Did Joe Biden serve in the military?

No. He received student deferments during the Vietnam War and was later classified as ineligible because of asthma he had as a teenager. His son Beau served in the Delaware Army National Guard and was deployed to Iraq.

What is Joe Biden's middle name?

Robinette, a name from his father's side of the family.

Who succeeded Joe Biden as President?

Donald Trump, who won the 2024 election, succeeded him on 20 January 2025.

Final Thoughts

Whatever your view of his politics, Joe Biden's life story is one of persistence through personal hardship: a boy who stuttered, a young senator who lost his wife and daughter, a father who lost a son and a politician who reached the presidency on his third attempt, nearly five decades after first entering the Senate. His career offers a window into half a century of American political history.
