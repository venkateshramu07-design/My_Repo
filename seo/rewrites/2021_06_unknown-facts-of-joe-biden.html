Quick answer: Joe Biden served as the 46th President of the United States from 2021 to 2025 and as Vice President under Barack Obama from 2009 to 2017. Lesser-known facts about him include his lifelong struggle with a stutter, his election to the US Senate at just 29, the car crash that killed his first wife and daughter weeks later, his decades of daily train commuting between Delaware and Washington, and his record as the oldest person ever to serve as US President.

Joe Biden spent more than half a century in American public life, yet many people know him only through headlines. Behind the political career is a personal story shaped by childhood challenges, family tragedy and an unusually long path to the presidency.

Here are some lesser-known facts about his life and career.

Quick Profile

  • Full name: Joseph Robinette Biden Jr.
  • Born: 20 November 1942, Scranton, Pennsylvania
  • Party: Democratic
  • US Senator for Delaware: 1973 to 2009
  • Vice President: 2009 to 2017, under Barack Obama
  • President: 20 January 2021 to 20 January 2025
  • Spouse: Jill Biden (married 1977); first wife Neilia Hunter Biden died in 1972

1. He Overcame a Childhood Stutter

Biden stuttered as a child and was teased by classmates, who nicknamed him "Dash" after the Morse code dash. He has described practising by reciting poetry by Yeats and Emerson in front of a mirror to control his speech. As an adult he has mentored young people who stutter and spoken openly about the experience.

2. His Family Moved to Delaware When He Was Young

Biden was born in Scranton, Pennsylvania, but his family moved to Delaware when he was about 10 after his father struggled to find work. His father eventually found steady employment as a car salesman. Biden often credited his father's line, "Champ, when you get knocked down, get up", as a guiding principle.

3. He Was Elected to the Senate at 29

In 1972 Biden ran for the US Senate in Delaware as a long-shot candidate and narrowly defeated a well-known incumbent. He was 29 on election day and turned 30, the constitutional minimum age for a senator, just before taking office. That made him one of the youngest people ever elected to the Senate.

4. Tragedy Struck Weeks After His First Election

In December 1972, just weeks after his election, his wife Neilia and their one-year-old daughter Naomi were killed in a car accident while Christmas shopping. His young sons, Beau and Hunter, were badly injured. Biden was sworn in to the Senate at his sons' hospital bedside.

5. He Commuted by Train for Decades

To be home with his sons each night, Biden commuted roughly 90 minutes each way by Amtrak between Wilmington and Washington throughout his Senate career. He became so associated with the line that he earned the nickname "Amtrak Joe", and Wilmington's station was later renamed the Joseph R. Biden Jr. Railroad Station.

6. He Survived Two Brain Aneurysms

In 1988 Biden suffered two brain aneurysms that required surgery. He was away from the Senate for several months while recovering, then returned to work.

7. He Ran for President Three Times

Biden first sought the presidency in 1988 and again in 2008, withdrawing early both times. His third campaign, in 2020, succeeded when he defeated incumbent Donald Trump.

8. He Lost His Son Beau to Cancer

Biden's eldest son, Beau, a former Attorney General of Delaware and Iraq War veteran, died of brain cancer in 2015 at age 46. Biden later wrote about the experience in his memoir Promise Me, Dad and, as Vice President, led the "Cancer Moonshot" initiative to speed up cancer research.

9. He Chaired Major Senate Committees

During his 36 years in the Senate, Biden chaired the Senate Judiciary Committee and the Senate Foreign Relations Committee. He was a principal author of the 1994 Violence Against Women Act, which he often called one of his proudest achievements.

10. He Received the Presidential Medal of Freedom as Vice President

In January 2017, President Obama surprised Biden by awarding him the Presidential Medal of Freedom with distinction, the nation's highest civilian honour, in a ceremony at the White House.

11. He Was the Oldest President in US History

Biden was 78 when he took office in 2021, making him the oldest person ever inaugurated as US President at that time. He initially ran for re-election in 2024 but withdrew in July 2024 and endorsed Vice President Kamala Harris as the Democratic nominee.

12. His Wife Kept Teaching While First Lady

Jill Biden, who holds a doctorate in education, continued teaching English at a community college while serving as First Lady, becoming the first First Lady to hold a paid job outside the White House during her husband's presidency.

13. He Is a Practising Catholic

Biden was the second Catholic President of the United States, after John F. Kennedy. He frequently spoke about how his faith helped him through personal losses.

Key Moments of the Biden Presidency

  • Leading the US response during the later stages of the COVID-19 pandemic, including a large-scale vaccination campaign.
  • Signing the American Rescue Plan, the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, the CHIPS and Science Act and the Inflation Reduction Act.
  • Overseeing the US withdrawal from Afghanistan in 2021.
  • Supporting Ukraine following Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022.
  • Appointing Ketanji Brown Jackson, the first Black woman on the US Supreme Court.

Frequently Asked Questions

How old is Joe Biden?

Joe Biden was born on 20 November 1942.

How many children does Joe Biden have?

He had four children: Beau, Hunter and Naomi with his first wife Neilia, and Ashley with Jill Biden. Naomi died in 1972 and Beau in 2015.

Which states is Joe Biden associated with?

He was born in Pennsylvania but built his entire political career in Delaware, which he represented in the Senate for 36 years.

Who succeeded Joe Biden as President?

Donald Trump, who won the 2024 election, succeeded him on 20 January 2025.

Final Thoughts

Whatever your view of his politics, Joe Biden's life story is one of persistence through personal hardship: a boy who stuttered, a young senator who lost his wife and daughter, a father who lost a son and a politician who reached the presidency on his third attempt, nearly five decades after first entering the Senate. His career offers a window into half a century of American political history.
