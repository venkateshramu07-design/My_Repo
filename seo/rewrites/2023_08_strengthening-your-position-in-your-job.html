Quick answer: How do you become indispensable at work? To strengthen your position at work, consistently deliver high-quality results on what matters most to your team, keep building in-demand skills (including digital and AI skills), make your contributions visible without bragging, build strong relationships with your manager, colleagues and senior leaders, find mentors and sponsors, solve problems proactively, adapt quickly to change, and keep a record of your achievements. Combine this with professional behaviour, reliability and emotional intelligence. Also protect yourself by maintaining your network outside the company, keeping your CV updated and building an emergency fund, because no job is completely secure.

In a world of restructurings, automation and economic uncertainty, many professionals worry about job security and career progress. Some colleagues seem to grow steadily, get the interesting projects and survive layoffs, while others feel stuck despite working hard. The difference often lies not only in effort but in how strategically people build their value, relationships and reputation.

This guide explains practical strategies to strengthen your position in your current job, increase your chances of promotion and make yourself more resilient whatever happens in the job market.

What Does a "Strong Position" at Work Mean?

  • Value: you contribute results that matter to the organisation.
  • Skills: your abilities are relevant, up to date and hard to replace.
  • Reputation: people see you as reliable, capable and trustworthy.
  • Relationships: you have allies, mentors and supporters across the organisation.
  • Visibility: decision-makers know what you contribute.
  • Options: you have opportunities inside and outside the organisation.

1. Understand What Matters Most

Hard work on low-priority tasks rarely builds a strong position. Make sure you understand:

  • Your organisation's strategy and goals
  • Your team's priorities and key performance indicators
  • What your manager is measured on and worried about
  • How your work connects to revenue, customers, costs or risk

Ask your manager directly: "What are the most important outcomes for our team this quarter, and where can I contribute most?"

2. Deliver Consistently High-Quality Work

Reliability is the foundation of a strong reputation.

  • Meet deadlines, or flag risks early if you can't.
  • Check your work for errors.
  • Follow through on commitments, big and small.
  • Communicate progress without being asked.
  • Go beyond the minimum on important tasks.

3. Keep Building Valuable Skills

The World Economic Forum's Future of Jobs Report 2025 estimated that around 39% of workers' core skills are expected to change by 2030. Continuous learning is essential.

  • Technical skills: deepen expertise in your field.
  • Digital and AI skills: learn to use data, automation and AI tools relevant to your role.
  • Communication: writing, presenting and persuading.
  • Problem-solving and critical thinking: handling complex, ambiguous problems.
  • Leadership and collaboration: influencing, mentoring and working across teams.
  • Business understanding: how your organisation makes money and competes.

Use company training, online courses, certifications, stretch projects and learning from colleagues.

4. Become a Problem-Solver

People who solve problems are valued. Instead of just reporting issues, bring possible solutions. Volunteer for challenging projects, improve processes, and look for ways to save time, money or effort.

5. Make Your Work Visible

Good work that no one knows about doesn't strengthen your position. Visibility isn't bragging; it's ensuring decision-makers understand your contributions.

  • Share regular updates with your manager, focusing on results and impact.
  • Present your work in team meetings when appropriate.
  • Credit colleagues generously while explaining your role.
  • Document achievements with numbers: "reduced processing time by 25%", "handled 40 client accounts".
  • Contribute ideas in meetings and internal forums.

6. Build a Strong Relationship With Your Manager

  • Understand their goals, pressures and preferred communication style.
  • Hold regular one-to-ones to discuss priorities, feedback and development.
  • Make their job easier by anticipating needs and solving problems.
  • Ask for feedback and act on it.
  • Discuss your career goals openly.

7. Build Relationships Across the Organisation

Strong networks inside the company help you get things done, learn about opportunities and gain support.

  • Get to know colleagues in other teams.
  • Help others generously.
  • Join cross-functional projects and committees.
  • Attend company events and informal gatherings.
  • Be respectful and professional with everyone, including junior staff.

8. Find Mentors and Sponsors

A mentor gives advice and guidance. A sponsor is a senior person who actively advocates for you, recommends you for opportunities and speaks up for you in rooms you're not in. Research by economist Sylvia Ann Hewlett and others has highlighted sponsorship as an important factor in career advancement. Build sponsor relationships by delivering excellent work, making your ambitions known and showing commitment to the sponsor's goals.

9. Show Adaptability

Organisations value people who embrace change. When new systems, structures or strategies arrive, be among the first to learn and help others adapt, rather than resisting.

10. Develop Emotional Intelligence

Emotional intelligence, the ability to understand and manage your own emotions and recognise others', helps you handle conflict, pressure and teamwork.

  • Stay calm under pressure.
  • Listen actively.
  • Give and receive feedback gracefully.
  • Show empathy for colleagues' challenges.

11. Take Initiative and Ownership

Treat your work as if you own the outcome. Take responsibility for mistakes, fix them and learn. Suggest improvements and lead small initiatives.

12. Maintain Professionalism and Integrity

Trust is hard to rebuild once lost. Avoid gossip, office politics and shortcuts that compromise ethics. Be honest, respect confidentiality and treat others fairly.

13. Look After Your Health and Energy

Burnout undermines performance. Protect sleep, exercise, take breaks and use your leave. Sustainable performance beats short bursts followed by exhaustion.

Keep an Achievement Log

Many professionals struggle to remember their accomplishments when appraisal time arrives. Keep a simple running document, sometimes called a "brag document" or achievement log, and update it every week or two. Record:

  • Projects completed and your specific role
  • Results with numbers where possible: revenue, savings, time saved, customers served, errors reduced
  • Problems you solved and how
  • Positive feedback from clients, colleagues or managers
  • Skills learned and certifications earned
  • Ways you helped colleagues or improved team processes

This log makes appraisals, promotion discussions and CV updates far easier, and reminds you of your value on difficult days.

Staying Visible in Remote and Hybrid Work

  • Turn your camera on for key meetings and contribute early in discussions.
  • Share concise written updates on progress and results.
  • Use office days for relationship-building, mentoring and collaboration.
  • Respond reliably within agreed timeframes.
  • Schedule regular check-ins with your manager and key stakeholders.
  • Participate in virtual social events and internal communities.

Working Alongside AI

AI tools are changing many roles. Rather than fearing them, learn how they can make you more productive: drafting documents, analysing data, summarising information or automating routine tasks. Professionals who combine domain expertise with the ability to use AI well, while checking outputs for accuracy, are likely to be more valuable. Focus also on skills AI can't easily replace, such as judgement, relationship-building, creativity, leadership and handling complex, ambiguous situations.

How to Ask for a Promotion

  1. Understand the criteria: what does the next level require in skills, scope and results?
  2. Gather evidence: use your achievement log to show you're already performing at or near that level.
  3. Choose the right time: ideally before budget and promotion decisions are made, not after.
  4. Frame it around value: explain how you'll contribute more in the larger role.
  5. Ask for feedback: if you're not ready, ask exactly what you need to demonstrate and by when.
  6. Follow up: agree on a plan and review progress regularly.

Handling a Setback, Such as a Poor Review

A disappointing review or missed promotion can feel discouraging, but how you respond can strengthen your position. Listen to the feedback without becoming defensive, ask for specific examples and expectations, create a clear improvement plan with your manager, and show progress over the following months. If you believe the review is unfair, raise your concerns calmly with evidence. Many successful professionals point to setbacks as turning points that pushed them to grow.

An Illustrative Example

Imagine an operations executive who has worked hard for three years but feels overlooked. They stay late, handle every request and rarely complain, yet promotions go to others. After a conversation with a mentor, they realise their manager doesn't fully know what they do, and much of their effort goes to low-priority requests.

They start a weekly email update to their manager highlighting results, such as a 20% reduction in order-processing errors after a process change they designed. They ask their manager about the team's top priorities and redirect their time accordingly. They volunteer for a cross-functional project with the finance team, take a short course in data analysis, and begin using automation tools to cut repetitive work.

A year later, they're seen as a problem-solver with skills others rely on, and they're promoted to team lead. This example is illustrative, but it reflects how focus, visibility, skills and relationships often matter as much as hard work.

A Quarterly Career Plan

  • Month 1: clarify priorities with your manager, update your achievement log, and choose one skill to develop.
  • Month 2: take on a stretch task or project, and connect with two people outside your team.
  • Month 3: share results, ask for feedback, review progress on your skill, and set goals for the next quarter.

Manage Your Time for Impact

Being busy isn't the same as being valuable. To spend more time on work that strengthens your position:

  • Protect deep work: block focused time for complex, high-value tasks, and turn off notifications during it.
  • Prioritise ruthlessly: sort tasks by importance and urgency, and push back politely on low-value requests.
  • Automate and simplify: use templates, checklists and tools to reduce repetitive work.
  • Batch small tasks: handle emails and admin in set periods rather than all day.
  • Review weekly: check whether your time matches your priorities.

Become the Go-To Person for Something

Developing a recognised area of expertise makes you memorable and valuable. It might be a technical skill, a product, a client relationship, a process or a tool. Learn it deeply, share your knowledge generously through short guides or internal sessions, and help colleagues solve problems in that area. Over time, people across the organisation will think of you when that topic arises, which builds both your reputation and your network.

Having Career Conversations With Your Manager

Many people wait for their manager to raise career development. Take the initiative. You might say: "I'd like to grow toward a team lead role in the next couple of years. What skills and experiences would make me a strong candidate, and are there opportunities in the team where I could develop them?" Follow up every few months to review progress. Managers are more likely to support people whose ambitions they understand.

Protecting Yourself in Uncertain Times

Even strong performers can lose jobs due to restructuring or economic shifts. Strengthen your position beyond your current employer:

  • Keep your CV and professional profiles updated.
  • Maintain relationships with former colleagues and industry contacts.
  • Build a professional reputation through industry events, writing or speaking.
  • Develop skills that are valued across companies, not just in one role.
  • Keep an emergency fund of several months' expenses.
  • Know your employment rights and contract terms.

Sharpen Your Communication Skills

Strong communicators are noticed and trusted. A few habits make a big difference:

  • Lead with the point: in emails and updates, start with the conclusion or request, then give supporting details.
  • Be concise: busy leaders value short, clear messages.
  • Tailor your message: senior leaders want impact and decisions; technical colleagues want detail.
  • Prepare for meetings: know what you want to contribute and which questions to ask.
  • Practise presenting: volunteer for small presentations to build confidence.
  • Write clearly: well-structured documents show clear thinking.

Build Trust With a New Manager

A change of manager can reset your position, for better or worse. In the first weeks, schedule a one-to-one to understand their priorities and working style, share a brief summary of your responsibilities and recent results, ask how they like to receive updates, and deliver quickly on early requests. Avoid assuming that your previous reputation automatically carries over; earn their trust through reliability and openness.

Signs Your Position May Be Weakening

  • You're left out of important meetings or decisions.
  • Your responsibilities are shrinking or being reassigned.
  • Your manager gives less feedback or seems distant.
  • Your skills are becoming less relevant to new tools or strategies.
  • The company or department is struggling financially.

If you notice these signs, act early: ask your manager for honest feedback, align your work with current priorities, upskill, strengthen relationships and quietly prepare external options.

Internal Moves Can Strengthen Your Career

Sometimes the best way to strengthen your position is to move within the organisation: to a growing team, a strategic project or a role that builds new skills. Internal moves let you keep your network, reputation and benefits while gaining fresh experience. Speak to your manager or HR about internal opportunities, and build relationships with leaders in areas that interest you.

Negotiating Pay and Recognition

A strong position also means being fairly rewarded. Research typical salaries for your role, industry and location, gather evidence of your contributions, and choose the right moment, often during appraisals or after a major achievement. Present your case calmly and focus on value. If salary increases aren't possible, consider negotiating other benefits: flexible work, training budgets, a title change or a clear timeline for review.

Build Your Professional Brand Outside the Company

  • Keep your professional networking profile updated with achievements.
  • Share insights, articles or lessons from your work, respecting confidentiality.
  • Attend industry events, webinars and meet-ups.
  • Join professional associations.
  • Stay in touch with former colleagues and managers.

A strong external reputation makes you more valuable to your current employer and gives you options if circumstances change.

Strengthening Your Position Early in Your Career

If you're new to the workforce, a few habits make an outsized difference:

  • Be curious: ask thoughtful questions and learn how the business works beyond your own tasks.
  • Take notes: write down instructions and feedback so you don't need to ask twice.
  • Be dependable on small tasks: reliability on small things earns trust for bigger ones.
  • Seek feedback early: ask "How could I have done this better?" after finishing tasks.
  • Build peer relationships: today's peers become tomorrow's leaders and referees.

Strengthening Your Position Mid-Career

Mid-career professionals face different challenges: staying current, avoiding complacency and moving into leadership. Focus on developing others, leading cross-functional initiatives, updating skills in fast-changing areas, building senior relationships and thinking strategically about where your industry is heading. Experience is valuable, but only when combined with adaptability and continued learning.

Common Mistakes That Weaken Your Position

  • Working hard on low-priority tasks
  • Assuming good work speaks for itself
  • Neglecting relationships
  • Resisting change
  • Stopping learning after mastering your current role
  • Over-promising and under-delivering
  • Engaging in gossip or negative politics
  • Being invisible in remote or hybrid settings

A Quick Self-Assessment

Rate yourself from 1 to 5 on each statement:

  • I know my team's top three priorities and spend most of my time on them.
  • My manager clearly understands my recent results.
  • I've learned a new, relevant skill in the past six months.
  • I have at least one mentor and one potential sponsor.
  • I have good relationships with people outside my immediate team.
  • Colleagues rely on me for something specific.
  • My CV and professional profile are up to date.
  • I have savings to cover several months of expenses.

Any statement you rate 1 or 2 points to a practical next step.

Frequently Asked Questions: How to Become Indispensable at Work

How can I become indispensable at work?

No one is truly indispensable, but you can become highly valuable by delivering important results, building rare and relevant skills, solving problems and maintaining strong relationships.

How do I get noticed by senior management?

Deliver high-impact work, communicate results clearly, contribute ideas, volunteer for visible projects and build relationships with mentors and sponsors.

How do I protect my job during layoffs?

Focus on high-priority work, make your contributions visible, stay adaptable and keep your skills current. At the same time, maintain your external network and savings.

What is the difference between a mentor and a sponsor?

A mentor gives advice; a sponsor uses their influence to advocate for your advancement.

How often should I update my skills?

Continuously. Aim to learn something new each quarter, especially skills linked to your organisation's strategy and industry trends.

Is it better to be a specialist or a generalist?

Many professionals benefit from being "T-shaped": deep expertise in one area combined with broad understanding of related areas. This makes you valuable both as an expert and as a collaborator.

How do I deal with office politics?

Stay professional and ethical, build genuine relationships, avoid gossip, understand how decisions are made, and focus on results. Healthy influence comes from trust and credibility.

How can I stand out in a large team?

Specialise in an area your team needs, take ownership of a visible project, share useful knowledge with colleagues and communicate your results clearly to your manager.

When is it time to look for a new job?

Consider moving if you've stopped learning, there's no realistic path to growth, your pay is well below market, the culture harms your wellbeing, or the company is in long-term decline. Ideally, start looking while still employed.

Does working longer hours strengthen my position?

Not necessarily. Results, reliability and relationships matter more than hours. Chronic overwork can lead to burnout and lower-quality work.

How do I recover my reputation after a mistake?

Own the mistake quickly, fix what you can, explain what you've learned and what you'll do differently, and then demonstrate reliability consistently over the following weeks.

Should I tell my manager I'm learning new skills?

Yes. Sharing what you're learning shows initiative and helps your manager match you with opportunities to apply new skills.

Final Thoughts

Strengthening your position at work is about more than working harder. It's about delivering results that matter, building valuable skills, making your contributions visible, nurturing relationships, adapting to change and acting with integrity. At the same time, building options outside your current job gives you security whatever happens. Invest in yourself steadily, and your career will become more resilient and rewarding.

If you found these tips useful, share them with a colleague who's working to grow their career.
