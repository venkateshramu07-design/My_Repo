## Step 3. Answer real questions (3 answers a week; Quora first, Reddit once your account has some history)

Search Quora (and later Reddit) for each question below. Answer fully in the post itself, then add the link as
"More detail and a table for every country:". Change the wording a little each time; never paste the same text twice.

**Q: "Which country has the highest minimum wage?"**
> Luxembourg has the highest statutory minimum wage, at about €2,771 a month for full-time work (roughly $3,100). Australia is close behind at A$26.44 an hour from July 2026, then the UK at £12.71 an hour. The US federal minimum is still $7.25, unchanged since 2009, though Washington state pays $17.13. Converted to a month of full-time work in dollars, the top five are Luxembourg, Australia, the UK, Ireland and Germany. I put the full comparison for 41 countries here: https://randomthoughtsever.blogspot.com/2026/10/minimum-wage-by-country-2026-hourly-and.html

**Q: "Is it cheaper to charge an electric car than buy petrol?"**
> For most people who can charge at home, yes, and more so this year because petrol jumped after February. At October 2026 US averages ($4.28 a gallon and 18.3¢ per kWh), 12,000 miles costs about $1,830 in a 28 mpg petrol car and about $630 charging at home. The exception is relying on rapid public chargers: in the UK they average 79p per kWh, which can cost more than petrol. There's a calculator for your own numbers here: https://randomthoughtsever.blogspot.com/2026/10/is-it-cheaper-to-charge-ev-or-fill-up.html

**Q: "How much of my money is protected if my bank fails?"**
> It depends on the country: $250,000 per depositor per bank (per ownership category) in the US, £120,000 in the UK since December 2025, €100,000 in every EU country, A$250,000 in Australia, C$100,000 in Canada and ₹5 lakh in India. Money held in payment apps such as PayPal or Wise is often e-money, not a deposit, so it usually isn't covered. Table for 30 countries: https://randomthoughtsever.blogspot.com/2026/10/deposit-insurance-limits-by-country.html

**Q: "Where can I retire abroad on Social Security of $2,000 a month?"**
> Check the visa rule first, then the cost of living. Panama and Costa Rica need a $1,000 lifetime pension, Portugal's D7 about €920 a month, Colombia about $1,300–1,600 and Thailand ฿65,000 (about $1,930) or a bank deposit. Spain (about €2,400 a month), Greece (€3,500) and Mexico (about $4,400–4,700) need more unless you qualify with savings. Comparison of 15 countries: https://randomthoughtsever.blogspot.com/2026/10/best-countries-to-retire-abroad-on-2000.html

**Q: "Which country has the highest income tax?"**
> For an average earner, counting income tax plus employee social security, Belgium takes the most (39.5% of pay), then Germany and Lithuania (38.7%). The OECD average is about 25%. Workers keep the most in Colombia, Chile, Costa Rica, Mexico and South Korea. The US is 24.3% and the UK 23.1% for a single person on the average wage. All 38 countries: https://randomthoughtsever.blogspot.com/2026/10/income-tax-by-country-2026-how-much-of.html
