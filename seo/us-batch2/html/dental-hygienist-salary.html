Quick answer: The median dental hygienist in the United States earns $98,100 a year, or $47.16 an hour, according to the latest official figures from the Bureau of Labor Statistics (May 2025 survey, published in 2026). The lowest-paid 10% earn under $74,880 and the highest-paid 10% earn over $126,050. Pay depends heavily on the state: the median is $137,220 in the District of Columbia and $61,160 in Alabama.

What the table shows

  • Highest pay: District of Columbia ($137,220), Alaska ($132,690), Washington ($131,160), California ($124,930), Oregon ($122,080).
  • Lowest pay: Alabama ($61,160), Mississippi ($67,280), West Virginia ($73,840), Kentucky ($78,830), Pennsylvania ($80,610).
  • Six figures: the median is $100,000 or more in 16 of the 50 places with data (49 states plus DC).
  • Best pay relative to local wages: Nevada (2.4x), Alaska (2.2x), California (2.1x), Oregon (2.1x), Oklahoma (2.1x). A high salary in a high-wage state can be worth less than a middling one where everything else pays less.
  • Biggest job markets: California (24,500), Texas (16,380), New York (13,660), Florida (11,980), Illinois (8,970).

Hourly Pay and Part-Time Work: What You Actually Take Home Before Tax

Most dental offices pay hygienists by the hour or by the day, and the BLS notes that many hygienists work part time, often for more than one dentist. At the national median of $47.16 an hour:

When you compare offers, compare the hourly rate and the guaranteed hours, then price the benefits. A part-time chair often comes without health insurance, paid leave or a retirement match, which can be worth several dollars an hour.

Starting Pay vs Experienced Pay

The BLS does not publish pay by years of experience. The 10th percentile is the usual stand-in for new graduates: $74,880 nationally ($36.00 an hour). In Florida it is $75,960, in Texas $71,530, in Illinois $74,280 and in California $102,090. The 90th percentile, $126,050 nationally, is what the best-paid experienced hygienists earn.

The gap between new and experienced pay is narrower than in most health careers: the top 10% earn about 1.7 times the bottom 10%. Location moves pay far more than seniority does.

What It Takes to Earn This Salary

  • Education: an associate's degree in dental hygiene from an accredited program is the standard route. The BLS says programs typically take 3 years to complete, because most require prerequisite science courses before the clinical years. Bachelor's and master's degrees exist and matter mainly for teaching, research and public health roles, not for chairside pay.
  • Licence: every state requires one. That means graduating from an accredited program, passing the National Board Dental Hygiene Examination and passing a clinical exam your state accepts.
  • Extra permits: many states license hygienists separately to give local anaesthesia or nitrous oxide. Holding one makes you more useful to an office, which is a fair point to raise when you negotiate.

How to Earn More as a Dental Hygienist

  • Check the next state or metro. The table above shows gaps of $30,000 or more between neighbouring states. If you live near a border, a second licence can pay for itself in weeks.
  • Ask for the hourly rate, then the hours. A higher rate with 24 guaranteed hours can pay less than a lower rate with 36.
  • Price the benefits. Health insurance, paid time off, a retirement match, continuing-education allowance and licence fees are all negotiable and all worth money.
  • Add the local anaesthesia permit where your state offers one, and say so when you negotiate.
  • Use temporary shifts to find the market rate. A few days through a staffing agency shows you quickly what local offices will pay.

Job Outlook to 2035

The BLS projects employment of dental hygienists to grow 8% from 2025 to 2035, which it rates "much faster than average": about 17,900 more jobs, to 243,200. It expects about 15,000 openings a year, most of them replacing hygienists who retire or leave the occupation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a dental hygienist make an hour?

The national median is $47.16 an hour. Half of hygienists earn between $40.18 and $52.15.

How much does a dental hygienist make with a 2-year degree?

The associate's degree is the standard qualification, so the figures on this page are what associate's-degree hygienists earn: a median of $98,100. A bachelor's degree does not usually raise chairside pay.

What is the starting salary for a dental hygienist in Florida?

The lowest-paid 10% of Florida hygienists, the best official guide to starting pay, earn about $75,960 a year. The Florida median is $91,980 ($44.22 an hour).

What is the average pay for a dental hygienist in Illinois?

The Illinois median is $96,670 a year ($46.48 an hour) and the mean is $91,820.

Who gets paid more, an RN or a dental hygienist?

Nationally they are almost level: $98,100 for hygienists and $97,550 for registered nurses. Nurses have more routes to higher pay (overtime, night differentials, advanced practice); hygienists usually have regular daytime hours.

Does it take 2 years to become a dental hygienist?

The clinical program is about two years, but with prerequisites the BLS puts the typical total at 3 years.

Which state pays dental hygienists the most?

District of Columbia has the highest median at $137,220, followed by Alaska ($132,690) and Washington ($131,160).

Is 25 too old to become a dental hygienist?

No. Programs admit career changers of any age, and the licence, not your age, is what employers hire on.

Why do dental hygienists leave the job?

The work is physically repetitive, with long periods leaning over patients, and many posts are part time without benefits. Both are worth weighing against the pay.
