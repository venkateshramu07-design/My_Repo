Updated 10 November 2026

Quick answer: Medicare is federal health insurance for people 65 and older, and for younger people with a qualifying disability, end-stage kidney disease or ALS. You qualify through work history, not income, and you pay premiums: the standard Part B premium is $202.90 a month in 2026. Medicaid is health coverage for people with low income and few assets, of any age, run by each state with federal money, and usually free or nearly free. In the 40 states and DC that expanded Medicaid, adults under 65 qualify with income up to 138% of the poverty line, about $22,025 a year for one person in 2026. Many people qualify for both, and then Medicaid pays most of what Medicare leaves.

Original Medicare (Parts A and B) has no annual out-of-pocket limit. That is why many people add a Medigap (Medicare Supplement) policy or choose a Medicare Advantage plan. CMS normally announces the 2027 Part A and B figures in mid-November; the Medicare Trustees projected a 2027 Part B premium of $209.50.

Medicaid: Who Qualifies

Every state must cover certain groups, including low-income children, pregnant women, some parents, and older or disabled people who receive SSI. Beyond that, the rules depend on the state.

The 10 states that have not expanded Medicaid are Alabama, Florida, Georgia, Kansas, Mississippi, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Wisconsin and Wyoming. In those states most adults without children cannot get Medicaid however low their income.

New from 2027: work requirements

Federal law now requires adults aged 19 to 64 who qualify through the Medicaid expansion to show 80 hours a month of work, job training, education or community service, starting by 1 January 2027 (states can ask for a delay to the end of 2028). Parents of young children, pregnant women, people with disabilities or serious medical conditions, and some others are exempt. It does not apply to people on Medicare or to older Medicaid enrollees.

Having Both: "Dual Eligible"

More than 12 million people are enrolled in both programs. Medicare pays first; Medicaid pays second and fills the gaps.

  • Full dual eligibility: Medicaid pays your Medicare premiums and most cost-sharing, and covers services Medicare doesn't, such as long-term care.
  • Medicare Savings Programs: if your income is a little too high for full Medicaid, your state may still pay your Part B premium, and at the lowest income level (QMB) your deductibles and coinsurance too. The QMB federal limit is 100% of the poverty line; many states set higher limits or disregard assets.
  • Extra Help: anyone in a Medicare Savings Program automatically gets Extra Help with Part D drug costs.
  • Special plans: Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans (D-SNPs) coordinate both programs in one Medicare Advantage plan.

Common Misconceptions

  • "Medicare is free." Part A usually is. Part B costs at least $202.90 a month in 2026, and there are deductibles and 20% coinsurance.
  • "Medicare pays for nursing homes." Only short skilled stays after a hospital admission. Long-term custodial care is paid by Medicaid, private funds or long-term care insurance.
  • "Medicaid is only for children and families." Older and disabled people account for a large share of Medicaid spending, mostly on long-term care.
  • "If I have Medicaid I can skip Medicare." When you become eligible for Medicare you are generally required to enrol, and Medicaid then pays second.
  • "Medicaid can take my house." While you or a spouse live there, the home is usually protected. States must try to recover long-term care costs from the estate after death, with exceptions for a surviving spouse and some relatives.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main difference between Medicare and Medicaid?

Medicare is based on age or disability and work history, and you pay premiums. Medicaid is based on low income, is run by each state, and costs little or nothing.

Who is eligible for Medicare?

People 65 or older who are citizens or have been lawful permanent residents for 5 years; younger people after 24 months of Social Security disability benefits; and people with end-stage renal disease or ALS.

Do US citizens get free Medicare?

Part A is free if you or your spouse paid Medicare taxes for about 10 years. Part B has a monthly premium for almost everyone, unless Medicaid or a Medicare Savings Program pays it.

Does everyone have to pay $202.90 a month for Medicare?

Most people pay the standard $202.90 in 2026. Higher earners pay more; people with low income can have it paid by their state.

What is the highest income to get Medicaid?

For adults in expansion states, 138% of the poverty line: about $22,025 a year for one person in 2026, more for larger households. Children's and pregnancy limits are often higher.

Can you have Medicare and Medicaid at the same time?

Yes. People with both are called dual eligibles, and Medicaid pays most of their Medicare costs.

What does Medicaid not cover?

It varies by state. Adult dental, vision and hearing are often limited. Services must usually be medically necessary and from providers who accept Medicaid.

How is Medicare funded?

Part A mainly by the 2.9% Medicare payroll tax shared by workers and employers; Parts B and D by premiums and general federal revenue.

What do seniors do if they can't afford Medicare?

Apply to their state for a Medicare Savings Program, Medicaid and Extra Help. Free counselling is available from each state's SHIP (State Health Insurance Assistance Program).
