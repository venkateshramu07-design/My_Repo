Quick answer: Breakup therapy is ordinary talk therapy focused on the end of a relationship. It helps you grieve the loss, stop ruminating and checking on your ex, rebuild your routine and self-worth, and understand patterns so the next relationship goes better. Most people do well with a short course of cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) or interpersonal therapy, often 6 to 12 sessions. It's worth seeking help sooner if low mood, poor sleep or anxiety are disrupting work or daily life for more than a few weeks, if you're drinking more to cope, or if the relationship involved abuse.

Is It Normal Heartbreak or Something More?

Sadness, crying, poor sleep and trouble concentrating are normal after a breakup, and they usually ease week by week. Consider professional help if:

  • low mood, hopelessness or loss of interest last most days for more than a couple of weeks;
  • you can't work, eat or sleep properly;
  • you're using alcohol, drugs, or risky behaviour to cope;
  • you're checking on, contacting or following your ex in ways you can't stop;
  • you feel unsafe, or the relationship involved violence, threats or control;
  • you have thoughts of harming yourself.

How to Find the Right Therapist

  • Look for a licensed therapist (psychologist, licensed professional counselor, marriage and family therapist or clinical social worker) who lists relationships, grief or divorce among their focus areas.
  • Ask about their approach (CBT, IPT and so on), how many sessions they expect, and how you'll know it's working.
  • Fit matters: it's fine to switch after one or two sessions if you don't feel understood.
  • Telehealth widens your choice, especially for evening appointments.
  • If you were abused, look for a therapist with domestic violence and trauma training, and contact the National Domestic Violence Hotline (1-800-799-7233) for safety planning.

What Therapy Costs, and Your Rights on Price

Session fees vary by region and by the therapist's licence. Before you book:

  • With insurance: mental health care must be covered no more restrictively than medical care under federal parity law. Check your copay or coinsurance, your deductible, and whether the therapist is in network.
  • Paying yourself: under the No Surprises Act you're entitled to a written Good Faith Estimate if you schedule at least 3 business days ahead or ask for one. If the bill comes in at least $400 above the estimate, you can dispute it within 120 days.
  • Lower-cost options: community mental health centers, university training clinics, employee assistance programs (often several free sessions) and sliding-scale practices.

What You Can Do This Week

  • Keep a simple routine: wake, meals, movement and bedtime at set times.
  • Limit contact and digital checking: mute, archive and remove reminders from your daily view.
  • Tell two or three people what you need (company, a walk, practical help).
  • Write down the reasons it ended, to read when you idealize the past.
  • Plan one small enjoyable or meaningful activity each day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I see a therapist after a breakup?

If the pain is making it hard to function, if it isn't easing after several weeks, or if you're coping in harmful ways, yes. Many people also find a few sessions useful even without those signs.

How long does breakup therapy take?

Often 6 to 12 sessions of a structured approach such as CBT; longer if there's depression, trauma or a long marriage involved.

Can therapy help me get my ex back?

Therapy helps you understand what happened and decide what you want, including whether reconciliation is healthy. Couples therapy is the option if both of you want to try again.

Is breakup therapy covered by insurance?

Insurance covers therapy for diagnosed conditions such as depression, anxiety or adjustment disorder, which are common after breakups. Check with your plan.

How long does it take to get over a breakup?

It varies widely with the length of the relationship and the circumstances; the worst usually eases over weeks to months, especially with support and routine.
