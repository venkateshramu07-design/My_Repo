Quick answer: ™ (trademark) can be used by anyone, on any brand name, logo or slogan they claim as their own. No filing is needed. ® (registered) may be used only after the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office has registered the mark, and only for the goods or services listed in that registration. To type them: on Windows, Alt+0153 gives ™ and Alt+0174 gives ® (using the number pad); on a Mac, Option+2 gives ™ and Option+R gives ®.

When You Can Use ® (and When You Can't)

  • Only after registration is issued. A pending application isn't enough: use ™ or ℠ until the registration certificate arrives.
  • Only for the goods or services in the registration. If your mark is registered for clothing, you can't put ® on the same name when used on coffee.
  • Only for federal registration. A state trademark registration doesn't entitle you to use ®.
  • Stop using it if the registration lapses or is cancelled.

Using ® on an unregistered mark is improper and can count against you: it can be treated as an attempt to mislead, and can harm your own application or a later lawsuit.

Why the ® is worth having

Under the Lanham Act (15 U.S.C. § 1111), a registrant gives notice of registration by showing ®, or the words "Registered in U.S. Patent and Trademark Office" or "Reg. U.S. Pat. & Tm. Off.". If you don't, then in an infringement suit you can't recover the infringer's profits or your damages unless the infringer actually knew the mark was registered.

Where to put the symbol

Place it right after the mark, usually as superscript (raised and smaller): Brand™ or Brand®. You don't need it on every mention. Common practice is the first or most prominent use on a page, a package or an ad, and on the logo itself.

How to Get the ®: Registering a Trademark

  1. Search the USPTO's trademark database for identical or similar marks used on related goods or services.
  2. File online with the USPTO: a base fee of $350 per class of goods or services. Surcharges apply if your application is missing information ($100 per class), if you write your own description instead of choosing one from the USPTO's ID Manual ($200 per class), and for each further 1,000 characters of your own description ($200).
  3. Examination: a USPTO examining attorney reviews the application and may issue an office action you must answer.
  4. Publication: if approved, the mark is published so others can oppose it within 30 days.
  5. Registration (if you're already using the mark) or a notice of allowance (if you filed on an intent-to-use basis, in which case you file proof of use before registration).

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use TM without registering?

Yes. Anyone can use ™ (or ℠ for services) to claim a brand. It costs nothing and doesn't need a filing.

Is it illegal to use ® without registration?

It's improper and can be treated as false marking. Courts and the USPTO have held it against owners, for example by refusing relief. Use ® only once the federal registration is issued.

What is the difference between ™ and ®?

™ claims rights in a mark. ® shows the mark is federally registered, and gives you stronger remedies in court.

Do I need to put ® every time I use my brand name?

No. Using it once prominently on each page, package or ad is common practice.

What does ℠ mean?

Service mark: the ™ equivalent for brands of services (banking, consulting, restaurants) rather than goods.

How much does it cost to register a trademark?

From $350 per class of goods or services with the USPTO when filed online using the ID Manual's descriptions, plus attorney fees if you hire one.

Is © the same as ®?

No. © is for copyright in creative works; ® is for registered trademarks (brands).
