Quick answer: The median respiratory therapist in the United States earns $82,280 a year, or $39.56 an hour, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics (May 2025 survey, the latest, published in 2026). The lowest-paid 10% earn under $63,660; the highest-paid 10% earn over $118,050. By state, the median runs from $111,950 in the District of Columbia to $63,270 in Mississippi.

What It Takes to Earn This Salary

  • An associate's degree in respiratory therapy from an accredited program is the usual entry route; some employers prefer a bachelor's.
  • Credentials: the entry-level CRT (Certified Respiratory Therapist) and the advanced RRT (Registered Respiratory Therapist), both through national exams. Many hospitals hire only RRTs or require the RRT within a set time.
  • A state licence: required in every state except Alaska, usually after passing the certification exam.
  • Basic and advanced life support certifications are standard hiring requirements.

How to Earn More

  • Get the RRT. It is the credential most hospitals pay for and many require.
  • Take nights, weekends and critical care. Shift differentials and ICU, neonatal (NICU) or transport roles add to base pay.
  • Look at travel contracts. Short-term hospital contracts pay above staff rates, with housing stipends, at the cost of stability and benefits.
  • Compare states. The median is more than $40,000 higher in the top-paying states than in the lowest.
  • Move up: department lead, educator and clinical-specialist roles pay more, as does a move into sleep medicine or pulmonary function testing management.

Job Outlook to 2035

The BLS projects respiratory therapist jobs to grow 9% from 2025 to 2035, "much faster than average": from 142,000 to about 154,100, with about 8,600 openings a year. An ageing population with more lung and heart disease drives most of the demand.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a respiratory therapist make an hour?

The national median is $39.56 an hour; half earn between $35.83 and $47.47.

What is the starting pay for a respiratory therapist?

The 10th percentile, $63,660 a year ($30.61 an hour), is the usual guide to a first job.

Which state pays respiratory therapists the most?

District of Columbia ($111,950), then New York ($107,810) and California ($104,820).

How much do respiratory therapists make in Texas?

The Texas median is $79,540 ($38.24 an hour).

How much do respiratory therapists make in Florida?

The Florida median is $81,160 ($39.02 an hour).

How much do respiratory therapists make in California?

The California median is $104,820 ($50.40 an hour), and California employs the most RTs.

Do respiratory therapists make more than nurses?

No, at the median: $82,280 against $97,550 for registered nurses. RT programs are usually shorter, though.

Can a respiratory therapist make six figures?

Yes. The top 25% earn over $98,730 and the top 10% over $118,050, and the median is above $100,000 in several states.

How long does it take to become a respiratory therapist?

An associate's degree program typically takes about two years, plus the time to pass the credentialing exams and get a state licence.
