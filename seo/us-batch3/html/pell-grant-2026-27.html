Updated November 2026

Quick answer: The maximum Federal Pell Grant for the 2026-27 school year is $7,395 (July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2027), unchanged from 2025-26. The smallest award is $740. Pell Grants don't have to be repaid. New this year: under the 2025 budget law (the One Big Beautiful Bill Act), any student whose Student Aid Index (SAI) is $14,790 or higher gets no Pell Grant at all.

Who Gets the Full $7,395

You qualify for the maximum automatically, whatever your SAI, if your parents (or you, if you're independent) were not required to file a federal tax return, or if adjusted gross income plus any foreign earned income excluded from tax is at or below:

  • 225% of the federal poverty guideline for a single parent;
  • 175% of the guideline for everyone else.

For 2026-27 the Department uses the 2024 poverty guidelines ($15,060 for one person plus $5,380 for each extra person in the 48 contiguous states; Alaska and Hawaii are higher). In dollars:

How Your Pell Grant Is Calculated

If you don't qualify automatically, the grant is the maximum minus your SAI, rounded to the nearest $5. If the result is under $740, there is no grant. The SAI comes from your FAFSA. Full-time, full-year examples:

What Changed for 2026-27

  • SAI ceiling: an SAI of $14,790 or more now disqualifies you. The exception is the Special Rule for children of service members and public safety officers who died in the line of duty.
  • Foreign income counts: foreign earned income you excluded from U.S. tax is now added to AGI when testing the poverty-line routes.
  • Workforce Pell: from July 1, 2026, Pell can pay for short job-training programs of at least 8 and fewer than 15 weeks (150 to 599 clock hours), provided the Department has approved the program. Programs must show at least 70% completion and 70% job placement. The first one was approved on August 4, 2026.
  • Amount: the maximum stays at $7,395, the same as in 2024-25 and 2025-26.

Who Can Get a Pell Grant

  • Undergraduates without a bachelor's degree (once you finish a bachelor's, Pell stops, even if you keep studying).
  • U.S. citizens and eligible non-citizens with a valid Social Security number.
  • Enrolled in an eligible program at a participating school (most accredited colleges, community colleges and trade schools).
  • Making satisfactory academic progress, and not in default on a federal student loan.
  • Under the 600% lifetime limit: six years of full-time awards. Part-time years use up less.

How to Apply and When the Money Arrives

  • File the FAFSA for 2026-27 at the official site (it uses your 2024 tax information). There is no separate Pell application.
  • Your school decides the amount using your SAI, cost of attendance and enrollment, and lists it in your aid offer.
  • It's paid to your school account at least once a term. Anything left after tuition, fees and on-campus charges is refunded to you for books and living costs.
  • Apply early. Pell itself isn't first-come, first-served, but many state and college grants that stack on top of it are.

Four-Year Value

A student on the maximum for four full-time years receives $29,580, before any summer Year-Round Pell. That uses 400% of the 600% lifetime limit, which leaves room for a fifth year or a transfer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the maximum Pell Grant for 2026-27?

$7,395 for a full-time student for the full year. The minimum is $740.

Is the Pell Grant going up in 2026?

No. Congress kept the maximum at $7,395 in the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026.

What SAI qualifies for a Pell Grant?

For a calculated award, an SAI of 6,655 or less. You can also qualify for the full or minimum award through the income tests above. An SAI of $14,790 or more disqualifies you in all cases except the Special Rule.

What income qualifies for a Pell Grant?

There's no single cutoff, because the SAI also reflects family size, assets and the number in college. As a guide, a two-parent family of four with AGI at or below $54,600 gets the full grant automatically.

Do you have to pay back a Pell Grant?

Usually not. You may owe part of it back if you withdraw early in a term, or if you received more than you were eligible for.

Can I get a Pell Grant for a short certificate program?

Yes, from July 1, 2026, if the program is Department-approved under Workforce Pell (8 to fewer than 15 weeks, 150 to 599 clock hours).

Can graduate students get a Pell Grant?

Generally no. Pell is for undergraduates without a bachelor's degree; a narrow exception exists for some teacher-certification programs.

Does a Pell Grant affect taxes?

Pell spent on tuition and required fees, books and supplies is tax-free. The part used for room and board is taxable income. You can choose to treat some Pell as taxable so you can claim the American Opportunity Tax Credit, which can leave you better off.
