Quick answer: A DSCR loan is a mortgage on a rental property that the lender approves on the property's rent instead of your personal income. DSCR stands for debt service coverage ratio: monthly rent divided by the monthly payment for principal, interest, taxes, insurance and any HOA dues (PITIA). A ratio of 1.00 means the rent exactly covers the payment; most lenders want 1.00 to 1.25 or more. Because the loans are for business purposes, they fall outside the federal ability-to-repay rules for home loans. Expect higher rates than a standard mortgage, a 20-25% down payment and a credit check, but no tax returns or pay stubs.

The DSCR Formula, Worked Out

DSCR = monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA

Example: a $350,000 single-family rental with 25% down, a $262,500 loan at 7.5% over 30 years, property tax of $4,200 a year, insurance of $1,800 a year and market rent of $2,800 a month.

How Lenders Read the Ratio

DSCRWhat it meansTypical lender view
Below 1.00Rent doesn't cover the paymentSome lenders still lend ("no-ratio" programs) with more down and a higher rate
1.00 - 1.19Break-even to thin marginAccepted by many, often with pricing add-ons
1.20 - 1.24ComfortableStandard approval
1.25 and aboveStrong cash flowBest pricing and highest loan amounts

Lenders usually take rent from an existing lease or from the appraiser's market-rent schedule (Fannie Mae Form 1007 for single-family homes). For short-term rentals, some accept 12 months of booking history or an industry rent estimate.

What You Need to Qualify

  • An investment property, not the home you live in: 1-4 unit homes, condos, townhomes, and sometimes 5-8 units or short-term rentals.
  • Down payment of about 20% to 25% for a purchase (more for weaker ratios or lower credit), or a cash-out refinance at similar loan-to-value limits.
  • Credit score, often 660-680 minimum, with better pricing above 720-740.
  • Cash reserves, often 3 to 12 months of PITIA in the bank after closing.
  • An entity (LLC) is usually allowed and often preferred, with a personal guarantee.
  • No personal income documents: no W-2s, tax returns or employment verification, which is why self-employed investors and those with many properties use them.

Costs and Catches

  • Rate premium: DSCR rates usually run above conventional investment-property rates, and more so for low ratios, low credit scores, short-term rentals or cash-out.
  • Prepayment penalties: often 5-4-3-2-1 (5% of the balance if repaid in year one, falling each year) or 3-2-1. You can usually pay a higher rate to shorten or remove them.
  • Points and fees: origination points, underwriting and appraisal fees, including the rent schedule.
  • Interest-only options raise the DSCR on paper but build no equity during the interest-only period.
  • Fewer consumer protections: because the loan is for business purposes, the federal ability-to-repay rule and some disclosure rules for consumer mortgages don't apply. Read the note carefully.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does DSCR stand for?

Debt service coverage ratio: the property's rental income divided by its debt payments (PITIA for residential DSCR loans).

What is a good DSCR for a rental property?

1.25 or higher gets the best terms from most lenders; 1.00 is the usual floor, though some programs go lower with more down.

Can I get a DSCR loan with no money down?

No. Expect at least 20% down for a purchase; some lenders go to 15% for strong ratios and credit.

Do DSCR loans check credit?

Yes. They skip income documents, not the credit check.

Can I use a DSCR loan for an Airbnb?

Many lenders offer short-term-rental DSCR programs that use booking history or market data for the rent figure, often with stricter terms.

Can I live in a property bought with a DSCR loan?

No. They're for investment properties only; living in it would breach the loan's occupancy terms.
