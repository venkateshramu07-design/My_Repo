Quick answer: A SIP (systematic investment plan) calculator estimates what a fixed monthly investment could grow to. For example, investing ₹10,000 a month for 15 years at an assumed 12% annual return would grow to about ₹50.5 lakh, of which ₹18 lakh is your own money and about ₹32.5 lakh is estimated returns. How to Use This SIP Calculator

  1. Monthly investment: the amount you plan to invest each month.
  2. Expected return: the average yearly return you assume. Equity mutual funds have historically delivered higher but more volatile returns than debt funds; many people test a range, such as 8%, 10% and 12%, rather than relying on one number.
  3. Time period: how many years you'll keep investing.
  4. Yearly step-up: how much you'll increase your SIP each year, for example 10% as your salary rises. Leave it at 0 for a flat SIP.
  5. Inflation: used to show what the final amount would be worth in today's money.

The results update instantly. Open the year-by-year table to see how the value grows, and notice how most of the growth happens in the later years. That's compounding at work.

The SIP Formula

For a flat SIP invested at the start of each month, the future value is:

FV = P × [((1 + r)n − 1) ÷ r] × (1 + r)

  • P is the monthly investment
  • r is the monthly rate of return (annual rate ÷ 12)
  • n is the number of months

With ₹10,000 a month, 12% a year (r = 1% a month) and 15 years (n = 180): 1.01180 ≈ 5.996, so FV ≈ 10,000 × 499.6 × 1.01 ≈ ₹50.46 lakh. The calculator uses the same approach, month by month, so it can also handle a yearly step-up.

Some calculators assume the investment is made at the end of each month, which gives a slightly lower figure. Neither is wrong; they just make different assumptions.

Worked Examples

The Power of Starting Early

Two friends each invest ₹5,000 a month, assuming 12% a year. One starts at 25 and invests until 60 (35 years). The other starts at 35 (25 years). Using the formula above, the first would have roughly ₹3.2 crore and the second roughly ₹95 lakh. The early starter invested ₹6 lakh more but ended up with more than three times as much, because the money had ten extra years to compound.

The Power of a Step-Up

A ₹10,000 monthly SIP for 20 years at 12% grows to about ₹1 crore. Increasing the SIP by 10% every year raises the final value to roughly ₹2 crore. Try it in the calculator: set 20 years and a 10% step-up.

Why Inflation Matters

₹50 lakh in 15 years won't buy what ₹50 lakh buys today. At 6% inflation, it would be worth about ₹21 lakh in today's money. Always check the "today's money" figure when planning goals like retirement or a child's education.

Tips for Getting the Most From a SIP

  • Be realistic about returns. Test lower returns as well as higher ones so a weak decade doesn't derail your plan.
  • Keep investing during market falls. SIPs buy more units when prices are low, which is called rupee cost averaging. Stopping during a fall gives up that benefit.
  • Step up with your income. Even a 5% to 10% yearly increase makes a big difference.
  • Match the fund to the goal. Equity funds suit long-term goals of seven years or more; shorter goals usually need lower-risk options.
  • Watch costs. Direct plans of mutual funds have lower expense ratios than regular plans, which adds up over decades.
  • Diversify. Our guide to portfolio diversification explains how to spread risk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the SIP calculator result guaranteed?

No. It's an estimate based on a constant return. Market-linked investments go up and down, and actual returns can be higher or lower.

What return should I assume?

There's no single right number. Many people test a range. Look at the fund category's long-term history, remember that past returns don't guarantee future ones, and be conservative for important goals.

Is SIP better than lumpsum?

It depends on whether you have a lump sum available and how comfortable you are with timing risk. Our comparison of lumpsum vs SIP explains the trade-offs.

Can I use this calculator outside India?

Yes. Choose $, € or £ as the currency. The maths is the same for any regular monthly investment, such as a 401(k) contribution or an ISA.

How is tax handled?

The calculator shows pre-tax values. Tax on mutual fund gains depends on the fund type, holding period and current rules in your country.
