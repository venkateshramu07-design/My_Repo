The EMI Formula Explained

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n ÷ ((1 + r)n − 1)

  • P is the loan amount (principal)
  • r is the monthly interest rate: the annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100. At 8.5%, r ≈ 0.00708
  • n is the number of monthly instalments: 20 years = 240

For ₹50 lakh at 8.5% for 20 years, the EMI is about ₹43,391. Over 240 months, you'd pay about ₹1.04 crore in total, of which about ₹54 lakh is interest.

Why Early EMIs Are Mostly Interest

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is highest at the start. In the first month of the example loan, about ₹35,400 of the ₹43,391 EMI goes to interest and only about ₹8,000 reduces the loan. As the balance falls, more of each EMI goes to principal. This is why prepaying early in a loan saves far more interest than prepaying near the end.

How Much Does Prepayment Save?

Using the example loan of ₹50 lakh at 8.5% for 20 years:

  • ₹5,000 extra every month: paid off in about 15½ years instead of 20, saving roughly ₹14 lakh in interest.
  • ₹10,000 extra every month: paid off in about 13 years, saving roughly ₹22 lakh.
  • A one-time ₹5 lakh prepayment in year 3: saves roughly ₹14.5 lakh and cuts more than 3½ years.

Prepay the Loan or Invest the Money?

This is one of the most common personal finance questions. Some guidelines:

  • Prepaying gives a guaranteed "return" equal to your loan interest rate, with no market risk.
  • Investing may earn more over the long term, especially in equity, but with no guarantee.
  • Tax matters. In India, home loan interest and principal may qualify for deductions under certain tax rules, which lowers the effective cost of the loan. Check the current rules for your tax regime.
  • Peace of mind counts. Many people value being debt-free, especially before retirement.
  • Keep an emergency fund first. Don't put every spare rupee into prepayment and leave yourself with no savings.

A common middle path is to split surplus money between prepayment and investing.

Other Ways to Reduce Your Loan Cost

  • Negotiate or switch lenders if your rate is well above current market rates. Balance transfers can save money but check fees.
  • Increase your EMI as your income grows.
  • Choose the shortest tenure you can comfortably afford.
  • Understand fixed vs floating rates. Our guide to fixed vs floating interest rates explains the trade-offs.

Home Loan Eligibility: How Much Can You Borrow?

Banks decide how big a home loan you can get with two limits. The first is your income: most lenders keep all your EMIs, including the new one, within about 40–60% of your net monthly income (the fixed obligation to income ratio, or FOIR), with the higher end for higher earners. The second is the property's value: RBI rules cap the loan at 90% of the value for loans up to ₹30 lakh, 80% for ₹30–75 lakh and 75% above ₹75 lakh, so you need the rest as a down payment.

Worked example: with a net income of ₹80,000 a month, no other loans and a 50% FOIR, a bank may allow an EMI of up to ₹40,000. At 8.5% for 20 years the EMI is about ₹868 per lakh, so ₹40,000 supports a loan of roughly ₹46 lakh. Enter the figures in the calculator above to test other rates and tenures; a longer tenure raises the loan you qualify for but adds a lot of interest.

Loan Against Property, Car and Personal Loan EMI

The same formula works for every loan with fixed monthly instalments, so you can use this calculator for any of them. Only the typical rate and tenure change:

  • Loan against property: usually 1–3 percentage points dearer than a home loan, with tenures up to about 15 years and loans of around 50–70% of the property's value. At 10.5% for 15 years the EMI is about ₹1,105 per lakh, so ₹25 lakh costs about ₹27,635 a month.
  • Car loan: typically 3–7 years. A shorter tenure means a higher EMI but much less interest.
  • Personal loan: unsecured, so the rate is higher, and tenures are usually 1–5 years. Check the processing fee too: it adds to the real cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are there charges for prepaying a home loan in India?

RBI rules don't allow banks to charge foreclosure or prepayment penalties on floating-rate home loans taken by individuals. Fixed-rate loans and other loan types may carry charges, so check your loan agreement.

Should I reduce EMI or tenure after prepaying?

Reducing tenure usually saves more interest. Reducing EMI eases monthly cash flow. This calculator assumes you keep the EMI and reduce the tenure.

Does the calculator handle floating rates?

It assumes a constant rate. If your rate changes, re-run the calculator with the new rate and the remaining balance and tenure.

What is a good EMI-to-income ratio?

Many lenders and planners suggest keeping total EMIs below about 40% to 50% of take-home pay, and lower is more comfortable.
